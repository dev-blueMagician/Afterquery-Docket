## Question

Of the currencies whose exchange rates American Tower assumed in both its February 2026 and July 2026 full-year outlooks, which did it assume had weakened the most against the U.S. dollar, in percentage terms?

## Answer

Peruvian sol

## Components

- c1: Peruvian sol

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 5: the February 2026 outlook is based on average foreign currency exchange rates to 1.00 U.S. Dollar for February 24, 2026 through December 31, 2026. It assumes 3.35 Peruvian Soles, 11.60 Ghanaian Cedis, 0.85 Euros, 134 Kenyan Shillings and 560 West African CFA Francs per dollar, and 1,550 Nigerian Naira.

Step 2. Doc 7: the July 2026 outlook's rates to 1.00 U.S. Dollar for July 28, 2026 through December 31, 2026 assume 3.45 Peruvian Soles, 11.80 Ghanaian Cedis, 0.86 Euros, 135 Kenyan Shillings, 560 West African CFA Francs and 1,380 Nigerian Naira. Doc 7's list no longer includes the Bangladeshi Taka or the Philippine Peso.

Step 3. Fifteen currencies appear in both lists. More units per dollar means a weaker currency, and only four rates rose: Peruvian sol (3.45 / 3.35 - 1) x 100 = 2.99%, Ghanaian cedi (11.80 / 11.60 - 1) x 100 = 1.72%, euro (0.86 / 0.85 - 1) x 100 = 1.18% and Kenyan shilling (135 / 134 - 1) x 100 = 0.75%. The CFA franc is unchanged and the other ten fell, meaning those currencies were assumed stronger.

Conclusion: the Peruvian sol was assumed to weaken the most.

Decoy: the largest move in the lists is the Nigerian naira, (1,380 / 1,550 - 1) x 100 = -10.97%, but fewer naira per dollar means the naira was assumed to strengthen.
