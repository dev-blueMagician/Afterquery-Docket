## Question

By how much did EOG's net debt increase from June 30, 2025 to June 30, 2026 in excess of the debt EOG said it expected to use to fund the Encino acquisition? Answer in millions of dollars.

## Answer

$499 million

## Components

- c1: $499 million

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 3 (May 30, 2025): EOG said it "currently expects to fund the acquisition through $3.5 billion of debt and $2.1 billion of cash on hand", so the debt it expected to use was $3.5 billion = 3,500 million dollars.
Step 2. Doc 7: the net debt table shows net debt (non-GAAP) of (980) at June 30, 2025, a negative amount because cash of 5,216 exceeded debt of 4,236, and net debt of 3,019 at June 30, 2026 (millions of dollars).
Step 3. Doc 7: moving from net cash of 980 to net debt of 3,019, net debt rose by 3,019 + 980 = 3,999 million dollars.
Step 4. Doc 3 and Doc 7: the rise in excess of the expected debt is 3,999 - 3,500 = 499 million dollars.
Decoy. Doc 7: reading (980) as a positive net debt of 980 understates the rise and makes the answer negative; the 3Q 2025 long-term debt borrowings of 3,472 are what EOG borrowed, not the debt it said it expected to use.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Document convention
How a careless solver fails: Rounding, scale or sign
Where the trap sits: Doc 7's net debt-to-total capitalization table prints net debt for June 30, 2025 as
"(980)". At that date EOG's cash of 5,216 exceeded its debt of 4,236, so the company held net cash, and
the parentheses mark a negative amount; the ratio row shows the same thing as "-3.5 %". The June 30, 2026
figure in the same row is a positive 3,019. Doc 3, the May 30, 2025 announcement, gives the planned
"$3.5 billion of debt" for the Encino acquisition.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: -$1,461 million. The solver reads (980) as a positive net debt
of 980, takes the rise as 3,019 - 980 = 2,039, and subtracts the $3.5 billion of planned debt: 2,039 -
3,500 = -1,461. It then reports that net debt grew $1,461 million less than the planned debt, or that it
did not exceed the plan at all.
Why the correct answer avoids it: A careful reader checks the sign against the rows above it. Debt of
4,236 less cash of 5,216 is -980, so EOG moved from net cash of 980 to net debt of 3,019. Net debt
therefore rose by 3,019 + 980 = 3,999 million dollars over the year. Subtracting the 3,500 million of debt
EOG said it expected to use leaves 3,999 - 3,500 = $499 million. The answer is positive: net debt grew by
more than the planned acquisition debt.
