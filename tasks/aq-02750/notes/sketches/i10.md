## Question

A letter takes four days to arrive. Counting from Federal Register publication, how many more days did someone commenting by post have to mail a timely comment on EOIR's December 2024 interim rule than on CDC's June 2026 Ebola order?

## Answer

20 days

## Evidence

- D02 (DATES, L31): "Electronic comments must be submitted, and written comments must be postmarked or otherwise indicate a shipping date on or before January 27, 2025."
- D02 (header, L5): "[Federal Register Volume 89, Number 248 (Friday, December 27, 2024)] [Rules and Regulations]"
- D05 (DATES, L27): "Written comments must be received on or before July 10, 2026."
- D05 (header, L5): "[Federal Register Volume 91, Number 121 (Thursday, June 25, 2026)] [Notices]"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h12. Asymmetric rule in two agencies' comment procedures: EOIR counts the postmark, CDC counts receipt. EOIR: published December 27, 2024, postmark by January 27, 2025: 31 days. CDC June order: published June 25, 2026, received by July 10, so mailed by July 6: 11 days. 31 - 11 = 20.
Ambiguity: both rules are explicit in the DATES lines. Treating both alike gives 16 either way (31 - 15 or 27 - 11); swapping them gives 12.
Necessity: D02 -> the postmark rule and its dates (D02 only). D05 -> the receipt rule and a 15-day publication-to-deadline window; D06 and D07 show the same rule and window for their orders, so they could stand in (declare as alternatives if written up).
After clarification: the wording gives the transit time only.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Mentions it vs does it; calculate across documents. Wrong routes 16 and 12.
Source risks: the scenario's transit time points straight at the two DATES lines; two unrelated rulemakings are compared.
Author calculation: date(2025,1,27) - date(2024,12,27) = 31; date(2026,7,10) - date(2026,6,25) = 15; 15 - 4 = 11; 31 - 11 = 20 (Python, 2026-10-10).
