## Question

On the Commission's figures, by how many percentage points did Z Energy's ROACE for its 2018 financial year exceed the top of the Commission's reasonable-return range? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

14.9 percentage points

## Evidence

- D11 (Appendix 2, line 935): "The Commission capital employed	1,006	1,101	1,425	1,463"
- D11 (Appendix 2, line 949): "The Commission net operating after tax	202	308	339"
- D11 (para 42.2, line 179): "the average capital employed used in the denominator (where earnings are the numerator) to calculate ROACE"
- D09 (X45.2, line 159): "well above our estimate of a reasonable return (6.9 - 8.6%)"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. Doc 9 prints Z's ROACE on the Commission's approach only as a 2016-2018 average ("approximately 22%"); the 2018 figure has to be built from Appendix 2's yearly rows, and the range it is measured against is only in Doc 1.
Ambiguity: 22% (D11 102) is the three-year average, fails "for its 2018 financial year" -> 13.4. Z's own rows (267 / 2,672.5 = 9.99%, D11 924 and 944) fail "on the Commission's figures" -> 1.4. 6.9 fails "top" -> 16.6. Doc 1's 20% (D09 159) is all fuel companies' return on new investment, not Z's ROACE -> 11.4. Capital convention: ROACE's denominator is average capital employed (D11 179), and only the mean of opening and closing balances reproduces the printed 2016-2018 averages (1,254 = mean of 1,053.5, 1,263 and 1,444; the three year-ends give 1,329.7, all four 1,248.75), so closing capital (339 / 1,463 = 23.17%, 14.6) is not the documents' measure. Whole points agree under every convention (15); the one-decimal form is what makes Doc 1's 8.6 irreplaceable (see necessity).
Necessity: Doc 9 -> the Commission's capital employed for 2017 and 2018 and its NOPAT for 2018 -> no other document carries any Z figure by year (searched "1,463", "339", "ROACE", "capital employed" in all eleven) -> the ratio is unreachable. After clarification: both documents still needed; the wording supplies neither the year's inputs nor the range.
