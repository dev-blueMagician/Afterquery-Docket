## Question

What combined share of the FTSE 350, to one decimal place, would ICAEW's individual cap allow the Big Four, and after how many of the yearly cuts in Nexia Smith & Williamson's sliding cap would that cap first be tighter?

## Answer

74.3% and 4 cuts

## Evidence

- D08 (Q20, L106): "Thus the market cap could start at 90%, and reduce by, say 5% per annum over 5 years."
- D08 (Q19, L99): "our preference would be for the number of companies audited as a starting base"
- D06 (Q19, para 87, L282): "each Big Four firm could be restricted to auditing 65 of the FTSE 350 companies"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h4. A per-firm count against a collective percentage path: 4 x 65 = 260 of 350 is 74.3%, which falls
between the path's third and fourth steps. ICAEW's 65 companies looks like Nexia's final 65%.
Ambiguity: (a) 5 cuts, or "never": 65 companies read as 65% -> fails "combined FTSE 350 share that the individual cap
allows". (b) "5% per annum" as percentage points (85, 80, 75, 70, 65) or as a rate (85.5, 81.2, 77.2, 73.3, 69.6): below
74.3% first at the fourth cut on both.
Necessity: D08 -> the path; D06 -> the 65-company cap. Neither is stated elsewhere.
After clarification: both still needed.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Unit normalization.
Source risks: answer is one of five small integers; each step is plain. BDO's 68.6% was not used because the two readings
of "5% per annum" then disagree.
Author calculation: 4 x 65 = 260; 260 / 350 = 74.3%; 90 - 5 x 3 = 75 (not tighter); 90 - 5 x 4 = 70 (tighter).
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: quick-test sketch only. 2026-10-12: first ask added (74.3% = 260 / 350) after
q01 rev 0's lone "7 years" was guessed with no documents; "4 cuts" alone is one of five answers.
