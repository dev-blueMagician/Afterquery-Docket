## Question

At the yearly rate of tender wins Deloitte's five-year target assumes, how many years of wins, to the nearest whole year, would bring the Big Four within ICAEW's individual cap?

## Answer

7 years

## Evidence

- D06 (Q19, para 87, L282): "each Big Four firm could be restricted to auditing 65 of the FTSE 350 companies"
- D02 (appendix 20.4, L616): "Firms outside the four largest currently perform 11 audits of the FTSE 350 today"
- D02 (appendix 20.4, L616): "firms other than the four largest would need to win about 60 tenders"
- D02 (appendix 20.4, L616): "in order to hold a 20% share of the FTSE 350 audit market in the next five years"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. A per-firm limit from one response turned into a number of audits that must change hands, then timed at
the yearly rate another response's own estimate implies. The audits the other firms already hold are a silent offset, and the
gold lies between the two answers the obvious short cuts give.
Ambiguity: (a) 8 years: all 90 audits outside the cap treated as wins still to come -> fails "tender wins": D02 L616 has the
other firms already performing 11, so 79 remain. (b) 6 years: five years scaled by 90/70 -> fails "yearly rate of tender wins":
the rate is 60 wins in five years, and wins are counted from the 11 held, not from zero. (c) 11 years: ICAEW's "around 60%"
(D06 L408) -> fails "individual cap": that figure is the collective cap; the individual one is 65 companies per firm (L282,
L53). (d) 8-9 years by adding D02 L91's "one to two years before these audits go live" -> fails "years of wins". (e) Which
present base: 11 (count), 10.5 (3%), 10 ("sevenfold"), 7 (98%) give 6.58, 6.68, 6.67, 6.59 years with each base's own rate:
7 on every one.
Necessity: D06 -> the 65-company individual cap; searched every document for per-firm limits: only D01 (60, "say 50"), a
different respondent's limit; without D06 the cap is unknown. D02 -> the 60 wins in five years (and the 11 held); searched
every document for tender counts: only D04 note 4 (8 of 29 switched, no forward estimate); without D02 there is no rate.
After clarification: the wording supplies neither the 65, the 60, the five-year count of wins nor the 11; both documents
still needed.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; State the rounding. Not a which-items question. Wrong
routes recomputed: 7.5 (8), 6.43 (6), 10.75 (11).
Source risks: "about 60" is approximate; a rate from the exact 59 (70 - 11) gives 6.69, still 7. "would bring" is read as
the least number of wins (every Big Four firm at its cap); a longer reading only raises the count.
Author calculation: Doc 6: 4 x 65 = 260 Big Four audits at most; 350 - 260 = 90 for other firms. Doc 2: 11 held today, so
90 - 11 = 79 wins; rate 60 / 5 = 12 a year. 79 / 12 = 6.58 years; 12 x 6 = 72, 79 - 72 = 7 wins left, over half a year's 12:
7 years to the nearest whole year. Script check 2026-10-12 (same values; alternates above).
