## Question

By how many billion dollars did the National Flood Insurance Program's claim payments for Hurricane Katrina exceed the total claims the program had paid over its entire history up to April 2005? Answer to one decimal place.

## Answer

$3.7 billion

## Evidence

- D09 (Background): "FEMA estimated that NFIP had paid over $15.7 billion in flood insurance claims from Hurricane Katrina as of August 31, 2007"
- D09 (footnote 21): "FEMA estimates that Hurricane Katrina alone accounts for over $15.7 billion in flood insurance claims."
- D02 (testimony of April 14, 2005, background): "Since its inception, the program has paid about $12 billion in insurance claims"

Steps:

1. Take NFIP's payments for Katrina alone, over $15.7 billion as of August 31, 2007 (D09), not the $16.2 billion for Katrina and Rita together or property-casualty insurers' $22.4 billion.
2. Take the program's cumulative claims paid since its 1968 start as reported in the April 14, 2005 testimony, about $12 billion (D02), not the 2004 storms' $1.3 billion or Isabel's $454.2 million.
3. Compute 15.7 - 12 = 3.7 billion dollars.
