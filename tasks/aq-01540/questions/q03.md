## Question

Among the defendants in eight federal rulings of September 11-15, 2026, which, if any, had asked the court to dismiss on forum non conveniens grounds but were dismissed from the case on a different ground instead? Where two or more qualify, name them in any order.

## Answer

Ofcom and OEC USA

## Components

- c1: Ofcom
- c2: OEC USA

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 7 (4chan and Lolcow v. Ofcom, D.D.C., September 15, 2026): Ofcom asked the court to dismiss on forum non conveniens and international comity grounds as well as on foreign sovereign immunity. The court addressed only immunity and dismissed the complaint for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction. Ofcom qualifies.

Step 2. Doc 1 (Advanced Technology Products v. Oriental Export Corporation, S.D. Ohio, September 11, 2026): one Motion to Dismiss was filed by both Oriental Export Corporation (OEC India) and OEC USA, Inc., and the Defendants moved to dismiss under Rules 12(b)(2) and 12(b)(6) "as well as the doctrine of forum non conveniens". The court granted OEC USA's request for dismissal under Rule 12(b)(2) and dismissed OEC USA without prejudice for lack of personal jurisdiction, so it never reached forum non conveniens for OEC USA. OEC USA qualifies.

Step 3. Exclusions. Doc 1: the same motion's forum non conveniens and personal-jurisdiction requests were denied as to OEC India, and only Count IX was dismissed, so OEC India stays in the case on Counts I to VIII. Doc 5: Royal Caribbean asked for forum non conveniens dismissal and was dismissed on exactly that ground. Doc 4: the Kansas defendants moved to transfer, not to dismiss, and DUAL's Rule 12(b)(6) motion was denied. Doc 2, Doc 3, Doc 6 and Doc 8 record no defendant's request for forum non conveniens dismissal; there the doctrine appears only in a plaintiffs' waiver or in cited precedent.

Answer: Ofcom and OEC USA.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Look-alike company
How a careless solver fails: Wrong members in a list
Where the trap sits: Doc 1, the Ohio ruling on the air-brake tubing dispute, where two similarly named companies, Oriental Export Corporation (OEC India) and its Delaware subsidiary OEC USA, Inc., filed one motion. The forum non conveniens part opens with "Defendants next argue that the Court should dismiss this case pursuant to the doctrine of forum non conveniens" and closes with "Defendants’ Motion is DENIED as to that issue", but its burden analysis names only the parent ("OEC India has not carried its ultimate burden to show that forum non conveniens dismissal is warranted"), a footnote speaks of "its forum non conveniens agument", and the conclusion reads "The Motion is DENIED as to OEC India’s request for dismissal under the doctrine of forum non conveniens." OEC USA is not named in that part at all, because the court had already dismissed it on personal jurisdiction.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: "Ofcom". The solver takes the conclusion's label and the burden analysis as the record of who asked, treats OEC India as the only company in the tubing case that sought forum non conveniens dismissal, sees that OEC India was not dismissed, and drops the tubing case, leaving only the regulator from the 4chan and Lolcow suit.
Why the correct answer avoids it: The careful reader asks who filed the request, not whose request was left to decide. The opening sentence says the Motion to Dismiss was "filed by Defendants Oriental Export Corporation (“OEC India”) and OEC USA, Inc.", the procedural history says "Defendants now move to dismiss ATP’s Complaint under Federal Rules of Civil Procedure 12(b)(2) and 12(b)(6), as well as the doctrine of forum non conveniens", and the court denies "Defendants’ Motion" on that issue. The later "OEC India’s request" wording follows from "Because the Court dismisses OEC USA for lack of personal jurisdiction". OEC USA asked, and was dismissed under Rule 12(b)(2), a different ground, so it belongs beside Ofcom.
