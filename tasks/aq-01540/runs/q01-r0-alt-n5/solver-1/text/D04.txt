LEGAL STANDARD
“District courts should approve consent decrees so long as they are not unconstitutional, unlawful, unreasonable, or contrary to public policy.” Stovall v. City of Cocoa, Fla., 117 F.3d 1238, 1240 (11th Cir. 1997) (citations omitted). A consent decree, however, “does not merely validate a compromise but, by virtue of its injunctive provisions, reaches into the future and has [a] continuing effect, [thus] its terms require more careful scrutiny.” Id. at 1242 (quoting U.S. v. City of Miami, Fla., 664 F.2d 435, 441 (5th Cir. 1981)). The Court must verify the terms are fair and determine “the proposal represents a reasonable factual and legal determination based on the facts of record,” which entails a record that is proven by evidence in the complaint, affidavits, or factual stipulations. Id. (quoting City of Miami, Fla., 664 F.2d at 441). A court, however, must examine [a consent judgment] carefully to ascertain not only that it is a fair settlement but also that it does not put the court’s sanction on and power behind a decree that violates [the] Constitution, statute, or jurisprudence. . . . [And i]f the decree also affects third parties, the court must be satisfied that the effect on them is neither unreasonable nor proscribed.

Id. (quoting City of Miami, Fla., 664 F.2d at 441) (emphasis omitted).
In entering any injunction, the order must include a statement of the reasons why it issued, terms of specificity, and “in reasonable detail . . . the act or acts restrained or required.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d)(1). The injunction can only bind “the parties[,] . . . the parties’ officers, agents, servants, employees, and attorneys[,] and” persons who are in active concert with a party and who receive actual notice. Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d)(2); see also Trump v. CASA, Inc., 606 U.S. 831, 851 (2025) (“The equitable tradition has long embraced the rule that courts generally may administer complete relief between the parties.”
(emphasis in original) (internal quotation omitted)). Thus, a court must verify the proposed injunctive language complies with Rule 65(d)’s requirements even with a consent Case 7:25-cv-00193-LAG Document 23 Filed 09/11/26 Page 4 of 11

5 judgment. See Combs v. Ryan’s Coal Co., Inc., 785 F.2d 970, 978 (11th Cir. 1986) (“while the preference is to enforce the requirements of Rule 65(d) ‘scrupulously,’ . . . [t]he appropriate inquiry is into the degree of specificity of the language of the decree in order to determine that appellate review is possible and that the contemnors knew their obligations.”). It is also the prerogative of the Court to keep jurisdiction in a case with a consent judgment and permanent injunction. See Spallone v. U.S., 493 U.S. 265, 276 (1990) (“In selecting a means to enforce the consent judgment, the [d]istrict [c]ourt [i]s entitled to rely on the axiom that courts have inherent power to enforce compliance with their lawful orders through civil contempt.” (internal quotation omitted)).
DISCUSSION
I. Complaint, Counterclaims and Consent Judgment
In accordance with the proposed consent judgment, all of Plaintiffs’ claims (Counts I–III) and two of Defendant’s counterclaims (Counterclaim Counts III and IV) are dismissed with prejudice; and two of Defendant’s counterclaims (Counterclaim Count I and II) are validated. (See Doc. 19-1 ¶¶ 9–10). With regard to the validity of Defendant’s Counterclaim Counts I and II the Court considers whether there is adequate factual and legal support. See Stovall, 117 F.3d at 1142; (Docs. 1, 10, 14). Counterclaim Counts I and II allege infringement on Defendant’s exclusive licensure of ‘016 Patent and ‘419 Patent under 35 U.S.C. § 271. (Doc. 10 ¶¶ 74–79, 80–85). Pursuant to 35 U.S.C. § 217,
(a) . . . whoever, without authority makes, uses, offers to sell, or sells any patented invention, within the United States or imports into the United States any patented invention during the term of the patent therefor, infringes the patent.

(b) Whoever actively induces infringement of a patent shall be liable as an infringer.

(c) Whoever offers to sell or sells within the United States or imports into the United States a component of a patented machine, manufacture, combination or composition, or a material or apparatus for use in practicing a patented process, constituting a material part of the invention, knowing the same to be especially made or especially adapted for use in an infringement of such patent, and not a staple article or Case 7:25-cv-00193-LAG Document 23 Filed 09/11/26 Page 5 of 11

6 commodity of commerce suitable for substantial noninfringing use, shall be liable as a contributory infringer.

II. Permanent Injunction
For the Court to order a permanent injunction as part of the consent judgment, the Proposed Consent Judgment and Permanent Injunction must contain, “the reasons why [the permanent injunction] issued; . . . state [the] terms specifically; and . . . describe in reasonable detail—and not by referring to the complaint or other document—the act or acts restrained or required.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d)(1). The Court issues this permanent injunction to protect Defendant’s rights as the exclusive licensee of the ‘016 Patent and ‘419 Patent and provides clear terms and details of the acts from which Plaintiffs are enjoined.
(Doc. 19-1 ¶¶ 8.a.–f.). The permanent injunction is enforceable against “Plaintiffs and their respective officers, agents, servants, employees, attorneys, affiliates, subsidiaries, successors, assigns, and all persons acting in active concert or participation with any of them, and all entities owned by or under common ownership with them,” and sufficiently is limited pursuant to Fed. R. Civ. P. 65(d)(2).
