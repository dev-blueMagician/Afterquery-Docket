## Question

Using the 2024 sick-pay consultation's figures, in which week would its break-even point fall at the median rate employee respondents suggested?

## Answer

Week 12

## Evidence

- D03 (Doc 5 L151, Impact on Employees): "Employees were generally supportive of a high percentage rate, with a median response of 90%."
- D05 (Doc 3 L143, paragraph 27): "the amount of time it takes for an employee earning £123 per week working five days per week to be entitled to less SSP under the percentage rate compared to their current entitlement"
- D05 (Doc 3 L105, paragraph 14a): "SSP is not payable for the first 3 qualifying days"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. The consultation defines its break-even point for one employee (£123 a week, five days)
against current entitlement (flat £116.75, three waiting days) and prints it only for 60/70/80%. The
employees' median suggestion (90%) is printed only in the government response. At 90% the weekly SSP is
123 x 90% = £110.70, below the flat rate; the current system's waiting days are worth 3 x 23.35 = £70.05;
the new rate loses 116.75 - 110.70 = £6.05 a week. 11 weeks lose £66.55 (still ahead), 12 weeks £72.60
(behind), so the break-even point falls in week 12 (continuous crossing 11.58 weeks).

Ambiguity: April 2025 flat rate £118.75 (Doc 5 L53) -> week 9 -> fails "the 2024 sick-pay consultation's
figures" (Doc 3 L125/L137 use £116.75). Employers' median 50% (Doc 5 L105) -> week 2 -> fails "employee
respondents". "12 weeks" vs "11.6 weeks" -> the question asks for the week. Mode 100% (Doc 5 L79) -> flat rate,
no break-even -> fails "median". Searched all five documents for "median", "break-even", "90%", "12 weeks".

Necessity: D03 (Doc 5): the only source of the employees' median (90%); "median" occurs elsewhere only in
Doc 2's cost annex. Without it the rate is unknown; a guess of 80% gives the printed 4 weeks. D05 (Doc 3):
break-even definition (£123, five days, current entitlement) and the flat rate and waiting days. D01 (Doc 4)
holds the same profile (case study 1, L409; "break-even point", L426) and the same rules (L45), so it is the
"or" alternative. Doc 5 has the flat rate but not the profile or the LEL value.

After clarification: the question states neither 90%, £123, five days, £116.75 nor the waiting days, so Doc 5
and one of Doc 3/Doc 4 remain indispensable.

Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Scatter the pieces. Wrong routes
(scratchpad breakeven.py): Table 1 pattern extended (60->2, 70->3, 80->4) gives 5; £118.75 gives 9; waiting
days as 3/7 of a week gives 9; employers' 50% gives 2; chosen 80% gives 4; no waiting days gives week 1.

Source risks: the profile (an LEL earner working five days) is guessable if Docs 3 and 4 are both removed.

Author calculation: 123 x 90% = 110.70; 116.75 / 5 = 23.35; 23.35 x 3 = 70.05; 116.75 - 110.70 = 6.05;
6.05 x 11 = 66.55 < 70.05; 6.05 x 12 = 72.60 > 70.05 -> week 12. Day level: day 58 (week 12, day 3).
