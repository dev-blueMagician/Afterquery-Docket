## Question

The Commission's 2022 proposal for the due diligence directive left several steps to the Commission's own discretion. Which of those steps does the directive as adopted oblige the Commission to take? List them in any order.

## Answer

Issuing due diligence guidelines, and issuing guidance on the fitness of industry and multi-stakeholder initiatives

## Evidence

- D01 (Article 13, L835): "may issue guidelines, including for specific sectors or specific adverse impacts"
- D01 (Article 14(4), L847): "The Commission, in collaboration with Member States, may issue guidance for assessing the fitness of industry schemes and multi-stakeholder initiatives."
- D01 (Article 14(3), L845): "The Commission may complement Member States’ support measures"
- D01 (Article 14(4), L847): "The Commission and the Member States may facilitate the dissemination of information on such schemes or initiatives and their outcome."
- D01 (Article 21(1), L951): "The Commission may invite Union agencies with relevant expertise"
- D02 (Article 19(1), L677): "shall issue guidelines, including general guidelines and sector-specific guidelines or guidelines for specific adverse impacts"
- D02 (Article 20(4), L719): "The Commission, in collaboration with Member States, shall issue guidance setting out fitness criteria and a methodology for companies to assess the fitness of industry and multi-stakeholder initiatives."
- D02 (Article 20(3), L715): "The Commission may complement Member State support measures"
- D02 (Article 20(4), L719): "The Commission and the Member States may facilitate the dissemination of information on such initiatives and their outcome."
- D02 (Article 28(1), L889): "The Commission may invite Union agencies with relevant expertise"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. The proposal's articles give the Commission five permissions ("may"): guidelines (Article 13), complementing support measures and devising new ones (14(3)), spreading information on schemes (14(4)), fitness guidance (14(4)), inviting agencies to the network (21(1)). The adopted directive turns two into duties ("shall": Articles 19(1), 20(4)) and keeps three as permissions (20(3), 20(4), 28(1)).
Ambiguity: model contractual clauses -> not left to discretion in the proposal (D01 Article 12 L829 "shall adopt guidance"; only the memorandum L179 says guidelines "may also" outline them). Helpdesk (D02 Article 21 L729), verifier guidance (Article 20(5) L723), secured turnover system (Article 28(2) L891), annex delegated acts (Article 3(2) L333) -> new in the adopted text, not steps the proposal left to discretion. Complementing support measures, spreading information, inviting agencies -> still "may" in D02. A reader may argue the helpdesk is one of the "new measures" the proposal let the Commission devise: D02 Article 20(3) keeps that permission word for word, so the discretionary step itself is unchanged.
Necessity: D01 -> which steps were discretionary (the "may" sentences; nothing in D02 or D03 says what the proposal left optional) -> without it the starting set is unknown. D02 -> which of them are now duties -> without it neither item can be confirmed and the three still-optional ones cannot be ruled out. D03 mentions "non-binding guidelines" only as a stakeholder view (L49).
