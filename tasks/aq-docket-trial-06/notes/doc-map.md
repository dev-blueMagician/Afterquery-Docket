- Operative definitions, rules and scope exceptions (verbatim anchors):
  - Report scope: "we limited our assessment of *depository institutions lacking federal deposit
    insurance* to state- chartered credit unions that purchase private primary deposit insurance"
    (155-157). "because banks, thrifts, and federally chartered credit unions generally are required
    to purchase federal deposit insurance"; "our analysis was limited to primary deposit insurance"
    (1179). Personal documents such as periodic statements were "not collect[ed]" (1213).
  - Section 43 can only reach state-chartered institutions: "Because all federally chartered
    depository institutions must have federal deposit insurance, section 43 can only apply to
    state- chartered institutions" (fn 59, 821). The shut-down provision covers "a depository
    institution (other than a bank)" (fn 47, 685).
  - Shut-down: no interstate commerce "unless the appropriate supervisor of the institution's
    charter state has determined that the institution meets all eligibility requirements for
    federal deposit insurance" (305-307; fn 28 quote 511-513). A one-time determination (507-509).
    Exemption "in consultation with the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation" (953; 315, 331).
    "self- activating" (1123).
  - Exceptions (fn 13, 311): FTC "may, by regulation or order, make exceptions for any depository
    institution that, within the United States, does not receive initial deposits of less than
    $100,000 from individuals who are citizens or residents of the United States, other than money
    received in connection with any draft or similar instrument issued to transmit money". For
    depositors "before June 19, 1994", the alternative is a "series of three notices" (311; fn 23 at 375).
  - Look-alike definition (fn 48, 687; 773): an entity FTC determines is "engaged in the business of
    receiving deposits" that "could reasonably be mistaken for a depository institution by the
    entity's current or prospective customers" (12 U.S.C. 1831t(f)(2)(B)).
  - Disclosure content: two facts, "not federally insured" and "if the institution fails, the
    federal government does not guarantee that depositors will get back their money" (301, 903,
    909-911). The rulemaking mandate is "the manner and content of disclosure required under the
    section" to "ensure that current and prospective customers understand the risks involved in
    forgoing federal deposit insurance" (325, 671, 909).
  - NCUA rules "do not apply to institutions that never were federally insured" (365). NCUA notice
    is event-specific and goes only to members at the time, whereas section 43 covers all depositors, ongoing (367).
  - FTC Act limits: FTC powers "generally do not extend to depository institutions* banks,
    thrifts, and federal credit unions" (341). The FTC Act "specifically excludes federally chartered
    credit unions"; "There is no specific exclusion for state- chartered credit unions"; the
    "corporation" definition covers only for-profit entities (fn 49, 689-691). Section 6 "expressly
    excludes the business of insurance ... except under very limited circumstances" (683; fn 50,
    693).
  - McCarran-Ferguson text quoted (817). The FTC Act applies to insurance "to the extent that such

### Operative definitions, rules and scope exceptions (verbatim, located)

- "dual banking system" because "banks can be either federally or state-chartered" (L189).
- Preemption regardless of label: a listed-subject or otherwise preempted state law "does not apply
  to national banks and their operating subsidiaries regardless of how the law is characterized"
  (L259).
- Test: state law that "obstructs, impairs, or conditions" a federally authorized activity (L247);
  App. II: "except where made applicable by federal law, state laws that 'obstruct, impair or
  condition' ... do not apply" (L569). Exceptions: state law applies if it has only "an incidental
  effect" or "is otherwise consistent with" National Bank Act powers (L247); contracts and property
  laws apply "to the extent that they only incidentally affect the exercise" of powers (L621).
- Visitorial powers: exclusive "unless otherwise provided by federal law" (L251); 12 U.S.C. 484
  makes banks subject to "visitorial powers vested in courts of justice" but, per OCC, does not let
  states exercise visitorial powers (L251, L633).
- Operating subsidiaries: "typically are state-chartered businesses" (L199); subject to the same
  laws as national banks (L199, L627) "unless a federal law specifically provides for state
  regulation" (L339). Affiliates = holding-company subsidiaries under common ownership/control
  (L199).
- 2004 guidance: OCC "does not regard referral of complaints by state officials as an exercise of
  supervisory powers by the states" (L293).
- Savings clauses preserve stricter state laws from preemption by federal consumer laws, but courts
  hold they "do not necessarily preserve such state laws from preemption by the National Bank Act"
  (L269).
- Express vs implied preemption (L575); field vs conflict preemption (L577); courts apply conflict
  analysis under the National Bank Act (L579); HOLA "field preemption" characterization (L609).
- Interstate Banking Act: host-state laws in four areas apply to out-of-state national bank
  branches "except when, among other things, federal law preempts" (L587).
- Section 1828(o): uniform real estate lending standards incl. "all real estate related laws and
  regulations" (L613). Pre-rule: "any state regulations outside of the five areas cited continue
  to apply to national banks, unless preempted by other regulation" (L615); 1995 revision (L619).
- Ceiling vs floor definitions (L469). Parity statutes (L421, L425); automatic trigger (L425).
- Consolidation vs conglomeration (L357). CAMELS scale 1 (best) to 5 (worst) (L739). Nonlead banks
  = banks "other than the largest national bank controlled by a company" (L743).
