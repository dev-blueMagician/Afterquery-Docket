## Question

In the 2011 wave of Osaka University's Preference Parameters Study, what was women's share of 40-to-64-year-olds who had a non-mandatory health check-up in the prior 12 months, minus their share of those aged 40 or over who had a non-breast, non-uterine cancer examination in that period? Give percentage points to one decimal.

## Answer

14.5 percentage points

## Evidence

- D04 (Table 4, check-up row): "|1|418|283|534|167|680|21|701|"
- D04 (Variables): "besides those organized by the government, employers, and schools—this is because such check-ups are mandatory"
- D05 (Table 4, zero row): "|0|723|588|1043|268|1276|35|1311|"
- D05 (Table 4, total row): "|Total|1347|1346|2028|665|2638|55|2693|"
- D05 (Variables): "summed data from all types of cancer, other than breast and uterine cancers"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. Composition of participants by sex in each Osaka panel study. Doc 3 (D04) Table 4: 418 of the 701 check-up takers are women; Doc 4 (D05) Table 4: women with at least one counted examination 1,347 - 723 = 624 of 2,693 - 1,311 = 1,382. Both tables print only within-sex column percentages.
Ambiguity: -9.3 (36.99% of women took a check-up minus 46.33% of women had an examination) -> fails "women's share of [those who had ...]": the printed percentages are shares of each sex column (D04 L213-215, D05 L88). -16.7 (36.99 - 53.67) -> same, plus the zero row. +1.2 (women's share of each whole sample, 51.18 - 50.02) -> fails "who had a ... check-up / examination". +4.5 (women's share of the unscreened, 55.15%) -> fails "who had a ... cancer examination". Searched all seven texts: no other source of PPS check-up or screening counts by sex.
Necessity: D04 -> 418 women of 701 takers -> D05 has no check-up data (its outcome is cancer examinations; D04's check-up question excludes cancer examinations, L78), D01-D03, D06-D07 use other surveys -> first share unknowable without D04. D05 -> 723 of 1,347 women and 1,311 of 2,693 respondents with zero examinations -> no other screening counts in the bundle -> second share unknowable without D05.
After clarification: the wording names the measures (non-mandatory check-up, non-breast non-uterine examination) and age bands; it supplies no count. Both documents stay indispensable.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Scatter the pieces. Not a which-items question; no rounding lever claimed (no rounded printed figure changes the one-decimal answer). Wrong-route results above.
Source risks: D04 L77 names only employer and school check-ups as excluded while L79 and L418 also exclude government ones; "non-mandatory" is the paper's own label for the measured variable (L149), so both readings point at the same 701. Samples differ (D04 dropped missing data and ages 65+; D05 ages 40-77), which the age bands state.
Author calculation: 418 / 701 = 59.629% (Doc 3); 1,347 - 723 = 624; 2,693 - 1,311 = 1,382; 624 / 1,382 = 45.152% (Doc 4); 59.629 - 45.152 = 14.477 -> 14.5 percentage points. Counts rebuilt from the printed percentages (36.99% x 1,130 = 418.0) give the same result.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: promote to q02 (stronger mechanism of the two: both halves need the composition, and the printed within-sex percentages answer a look-alike question).
