## Question

On each survey's own literacy test, how many percentage points higher was the average share of questions answered correctly by all non-student respondents to the Central Council for Financial Services Information's Financial Literacy Survey 2016 in Japan than by households in the China Household Finance Survey's 2015 wave? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

9.2 percentage points

## Components

- c1: 9.2 percentage points

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 5: Japan's survey measured financial literacy as the number of correct answers out of 25 questions. The study excluded 1,212 students, leaving 23,788 non-student respondents.
Step 2. Doc 5 (gender decomposition table, N 23,788 with 11,658 men and 12,130 women): men averaged 14.719 correct answers and women 13.480. 11,658 × 14.719 = 171,594.102
Step 3. Doc 5: 12,130 × 13.480 = 163,512.4
Step 4. Doc 5: 171,594.102 + 163,512.4 = 335,106.502
Step 5. Doc 5: 335,106.502 / 23,788 = 14.0872 correct answers per non-student respondent
Step 6. Doc 5: 14.0872 / 25 = 56.349% of the questions answered correctly
Step 7. Doc 2: for the 2015 China Household Finance Survey, the mean number of the three questions answered correctly was 1.414. 1.414 / 3 = 47.133%
Step 8. Doc 5 and Doc 2: 56.349% - 47.133% = 9.2 percentage points (to one decimal place)
Step 9. Doc 5: the descriptive table's financial literacy mean of 15.2 covers only the 15,228 complete cases (non-students with no missing education, self-rating, income or asset data), so it is not the mean for all 23,788 non-student respondents.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Part vs total
How a careless solver fails: Picks the wrong entity or section
Where the trap sits: Doc 5, the study of age, gender and financial literacy in Japan. Its sample paragraph begins by
excluding 1,212 students, says "Among the remaining 23,788 respondents" some lacked education, self-rating, income or asset
information, and then states "Thus, the final usable number of complete cases equalled 15,228 individuals. Table 2 shows the
descriptive statistics by gender." Table 2, which follows that paragraph under the title "Descriptive statistics for complete
cases", prints the only overall financial literacy mean in the paper: a "Financial literacy" row with N 15,228, mean 15.2
and standard deviation 6.6. It carries the asked measure's own label and comes straight after the paragraph that excludes
the students, so it looks like the non-student figure.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: 13.7 percentage points. The solver takes 15.2 correct answers as the
non-student mean, converts it to 15.2 / 25 = 60.8% of the 25 questions, takes Doc 2's 2015 mean of 1.414 of three questions
(47.133%), and subtracts: 60.8 - 47.133 = 13.667, or 13.7 at one decimal place.
Why the correct answer avoids it: A careful reader checks the N beside the 15.2: it is 15,228, the complete cases,
not all 23,788 non-students, as the question asks. The other 8,560 non-students, who lacked education, self-rating, income or
asset information, are left out of Table 2 but kept in the paper's regressions and decomposition, which use all 23,788
non-students (11,658 men and 12,130 women) and impute the missing items. The literacy score is not among the items the paper lists as missing, so the decomposition's
means are averages over all 23,788 non-students. That table gives 14.719 for men and 13.480 for women, with N 23,788 (11,658
men, 12,130 women). Weighting them gives 14.0872 correct answers, 56.349% of the questions, and 56.349 - 47.133 = 9.216,
which is 9.2 percentage points at one decimal place.

### Failure mode 2
Trap: Figure that must be computed
How a careless solver fails: Calculation slip
Where the trap sits: Doc 5's gender decomposition table (Table 5) is the only place that reports financial literacy
means for all non-student respondents. It prints two of them in its header, "Men: 14.719 (0.067)Women: 13.480 (0.060)",
and gives the group sizes only in a separate N row at the foot of the table, "23,788 (Men: 11,658, Women: 12,130)". No
overall non-student mean is printed anywhere in the paper, so the solver has to build one from these two rows.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: 9.3 percentage points. The solver averages the two printed means without
weights, as if the two groups were the same size: (14.719 + 13.480) / 2 = 14.0995 correct answers, which is 56.398% of the
25 questions. Against Doc 2's 47.133%, that gives 56.398 - 47.133 = 9.265, or 9.3 at one decimal place.
Why the correct answer avoids it: Women outnumber men among the non-students (12,130 against 11,658), so the overall
mean must weight each sex by its own count in the 23,788: (11,658 x 14.719 + 12,130 x 13.480) / 23,788 = 14.0872. Without
rounding it first, that is 56.349% of the questions, and 56.349 - 47.133 = 9.216, which is 9.2 at the one decimal place the question
asks for. Using Doc 2's distribution instead of its printed mean (1.4142 correct answers) gives 9.209, still 9.2.
