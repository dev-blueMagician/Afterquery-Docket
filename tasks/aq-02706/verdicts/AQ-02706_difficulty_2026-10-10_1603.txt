From the final rule (Document 2), the Regulatory Flexibility Act discussion addresses the SBA's Office of Advocacy comment:

- The OCC noted that, at the time, it supervised 1,040 institutions, of which approximately 636 were small entities. The SBA's Office of Advocacy pointed out that all 636 small entities could be affected by the proposed rule (to the extent they might submit business combination applications).
- The final rule's own estimate, based on five years of historical merger application data, was that 78 OCC-supervised small institutions could be affected in a given year (assuming all merger applications came from small banks).

636 − 78 = 558

The SBA's Office of Advocacy's maximum estimate (636) exceeded the final rule's annual small-entity impact estimate (78) by 558 entities.

Reasoning
---------

Let me trace through the documents to find the relevant numbers.

From Document 2 (the OCC's final rule on Business Combinations Under the Bank Merger Act, September 25, 2024):

In the Regulatory Flexibility Act section, discussing the SBA's Office of Advocacy comments:

"The OCC currently supervises 1,040 institutions... of which approximately 636 are small entities. As the SBA's Office of Advocacy noted, all of the 636 small entities may have been impacted by the proposed rule to the extent that they elected to submit applications to the OCC for approval of business combination activities."

And: "However, in practice and based on the number of merger applications that the OCC has received annually over the past five years, the agency expects the annual impact of the final rulemaking could be 78 OCC-supervised small institutions in a given year, assuming that all merger applications are submitted by small banks."

So the SBA's Office of Advocacy said it could affect 636 small entities (all of the OCC's small entities).

The final rule's estimate of small institutions affected in a year: 78.

636 - 78 = 558.

The answer is 558.
