## Question

Between the first and last voice rates in its 2011 determination, did New Zealand's mobile termination rate fall by a larger or smaller percentage than Australia's did in 2015, and by how many percentage points, to one decimal place?

## Answer

Smaller, by 0.4 percentage points

## Evidence

- D05 (Table 6, L845): "Effective from 6 May 2011 1 Oct 2011 1 Apr 2012 1 Apr 2013 1 Apr 2014 Voice (cpm) 7.48 5.88 3.97 3.72 3.56"
- D09 (para 44, L138): "In 2015 the ACCC more than halved Australian mobile termination rates to 1.7 cents from 3.6 cents"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h4. Both regulators "more than halved" their rates; which cut is larger turns on the first decimal.
Ambiguity: the absolute cuts (3.92 against 1.9 cents) -> fail "percentage". A New Zealand start other than 7.48 -> fails "first ... voice rates in its 2011 determination" (Table 6's first column). The SMS rate 0.06 -> fails "voice".
Necessity: Doc 1 -> 7.48, the first rate -> only in Table 6 (Doc 2, Doc 4 and Doc 9 give only 3.56). Doc 4 or Doc 9 -> Australia's 3.6 -> only in Vocus's two submissions (Doc 2 gives only A1.7cpm).
After clarification: Doc 1 and one Vocus submission still needed.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: State the rounding; sign. Rounded to whole percents the gap reads 1 point (52 against 53); at one decimal 0.4 by either order of rounding (52.4 against 52.8; exact gap 0.372).
Source risks: Australia's rate is in Australian cents; percentages need no conversion. Vocus's sentence is its paraphrase of the ACCC.
Author calculation: (7.48 - 3.56) / 7.48 = 52.406 percent; (3.6 - 1.7) / 3.6 = 52.778 percent; gap 0.372, 0.4 points; New Zealand's is the smaller.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: parked alternate; quick test only, not written up.
