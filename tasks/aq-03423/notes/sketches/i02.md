## Question

Over a first year, how much more or less per included gigabyte, to the nearest cent, does the 10GB plan 2degrees cites cost than its 25GB plan that the Commerce Commission priced?

## Answer

$1.60 more per gigabyte

## Evidence

- D05 (para 100, L550): "with data allowances of up to 25GB, for $65 per month (Skinny Direct) and $70 per month (2degrees)"
- D08 (answer on Q5, L335): "2degrees currently offers $55 for 10GB (with double data, i.e. 20GB, for the first 3 months)"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h2. 2degrees answers the Commission's price for its 25GB plan with a cheaper monthly price for a smaller plan. Per gigabyte the cited plan is dearer, and the size of the gap depends on the three months of double data: four easy steps asked as one figure.
Ambiguity: $2.70 more (double data ignored) and $0.05 less (double data all year) -> fail "over a first year": Doc 7 L335 limits the 20GB to "the first 3 months". $1.80 more (Skinny Direct's $65) -> fails "2degrees plan": Doc 1 L550 gives $70 to 2degrees. $2.01 more (mean of twelve monthly unit prices) -> fails "per included gigabyte" over the year, which divides the year's price by the year's data. 2degrees' $129 unlimited plan (Doc 7 L337) is not a 10GB plan.
Necessity: Doc 1 -> the $70 price of 2degrees' 25GB plan -> not in Doc 7, which lists its 25GB bundle without a price, nor elsewhere (searched "$70", "25GB") -> the comparison plan has no price without it. Doc 7 -> $55, 10GB and the three-month double data -> printed only at L335 -> the cited plan is unknown without it.
After clarification: both documents still needed; the wording gives plan sizes only, no price and no promotion.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; sign. Wrong routes recomputed by script (Fractions): 2.70, -0.05, 1.80, 2.0125. None equals 1.60, and one has the opposite sign.
Source risks: "$55 for 10GB" carries no "per month"; it answers the Commission's monthly prices and its bonus runs by the month.
Author calculation: Doc 7 data 3 x 20 + 9 x 10 = 150 GB; price 12 x 55 = 660 dollars; 660 / 150 = 4.40 dollars per GB. Doc 1 70 / 25 = 2.80 dollars per GB. 4.40 - 2.80 = 1.60 dollars per GB, exact.
