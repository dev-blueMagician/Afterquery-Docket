## Question

Against the Australian and OECD-average prices in the Commerce Commission's benchmark basket for unlimited calls with 20GB, by what percentage is the unlimited plan 2degrees calls a relevant comparison dearer or cheaper than each, to the nearest percent?

## Answer

130% dearer than the Australian price and 35% cheaper than the OECD average

## Evidence

- D05 (Table 5, L543): "unlimited calls + 20GB 18/30"
- D08 (answer on Q5, L337): "$129 unlimited GB mobile plan (which is not throttled), is a relevant comparison"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h12. 2degrees says its $129 plan is "considerably cheaper than other OECD and Australian offerings". On the Commission's benchmark row (New Zealand 65, Australia 56, OECD average 198, in NZD at purchasing power parity) it is cheaper than the OECD average and more than twice the Australian price: the two answers have opposite signs.
Ambiguity: the printed 17% and -67% (Doc 1 L543) -> fail "the unlimited plan 2degrees calls a relevant comparison": they are the benchmarked New Zealand price of 65. Australia's 22 in the five smaller baskets -> fails "unlimited calls with 20GB". Table 5's header row is scrambled in the text; the columns are fixed by the row itself (65 against 56 is +16%, printed 17%; 65 against 198 is -67%).
Necessity: Doc 1 -> 56 and 198 -> only in Table 5. Doc 7 -> $129 and its status as 2degrees' chosen comparison -> only at L337 (Doc 1 gives no price for 2degrees' unlimited plan).
After clarification: both still needed.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; State the rounding (sign). Wrong routes: 17% and -67% (printed); 98% dearer than the New Zealand price (65).
Source risks: the benchmark basket is 20GB and the plan is unlimited data; the comparison is 2degrees' own ("a relevant comparison"). Foreign prices are in NZD at purchasing power parity, the basis on which the table itself sets New Zealand's 65 beside them.
Author calculation: (129 - 56) / 56 = 130.4%; (129 - 198) / 198 = -34.8%.
