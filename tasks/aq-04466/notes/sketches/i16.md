## Question

The responsible entity of a managed investment scheme holds 40% of the scheme's units and votes them all against a resolution to replace it. Members holding 35% of the units vote for the resolution, and no one else votes. Under the current rules, would the resolution pass if the scheme were listed, and would it pass if it were unlisted?

## Answer

No if listed, yes if unlisted

## Evidence

- D04 (answer 14, listed schemes): "in the context of listed schemes, a vote on the removal of the responsible entity is by way of ordinary resolution"
- D04 (answer 14, unlisted schemes): "the removal of a responsible entity of an unlisted scheme is by way of extraordinary resolution"
- D04 (answer 14, the Voting Restriction): "from voting their interests on a resolution to remove the responsible entity of an unlisted scheme (the Voting Restriction), which restriction falls away in the context of listed schemes"
- D04 (answer 14, its effect): "it reduces the number of votes required to pass an extraordinary resolution"
- D12 (answer 14): "there has been a clear majority of the votes"
- D12 (answer 14, the second limb): "not being a majority of votes that can be cast"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h15, 2026-10-15 (round 5, playbook kind "an exception in one of two parallel rules"). Two
parallel removal rules: listed schemes by ordinary resolution, unlisted by extraordinary resolution. Only the
unlisted rule carries the Voting Restriction (the responsible entity and its associates may not vote their
interests); Allens says it "falls away in the context of listed schemes". BoardRoom gives the extraordinary
resolution's two limbs: a majority of the votes cast, and a majority of the votes that can be cast.

Hard design (playbook kind): exception in one of two parallel rules. The step no word of the question asks
for: whether the responsible entity's own 40% counts. Listed: it counts (the restriction falls away): 35 for,
40 against, no majority, fails. Unlisted: it does not count: 35 for, none against; the votes that can be cast
are the other members' 60%, and 35 is more than half of 60, so both limbs pass. Lure (importing the
restriction into the listed rule, the general expectation that a responsible entity cannot vote on its own
removal): "Yes, yes". Second lure (ignoring the restriction in the unlisted rule, or AIMA's "very
significant hurdle"): "No, no". The gold is the counter-intuitive pair: the same vote fails under the lower
listed threshold and passes under the higher unlisted one. The question avoids "associates", "restriction",
"s253E", "ordinary" and "extraordinary".

Ambiguity (readings reported, not cleared):
- "Yes, yes": the restriction applied to the listed scheme too. Rejected by Allens' "which restriction falls
  away in the context of listed schemes" (D04 l563).
- "No, no": the responsible entity's units counted in the unlisted scheme's votes that can be cast (35 of 100
  is not a majority). Rejected by the Voting Restriction and its stated effect, "it reduces the number of
  votes required to pass an extraordinary resolution" (D04 l563-l567).
- Ordinary resolution's threshold: the bundle defines it only by contrast (BoardRoom would drop the second
  limb to get an ordinary resolution; CAMAC's "simple majority of the votes ... cast", D04 footnote 47). Any
  majority-of-votes-cast reading gives 35 against 40: fails. A majority of all units would also fail.
- Extraordinary resolution's second limb: BoardRoom's "a majority of votes that can be cast"; the statute's
  "at least 50%" (not in the bundle) gives the same result (35 of 60 is 58%).
- Quorum: no current quorum rule appears in the bundle (AIMA proposes one, l314); none applied.
- AIMA (D02 l304) says associates "may therefore be prevented from voting as a consequence of the operation of
  section 253E" for unlisted schemes and gives no listed exception; it does not contradict Allens.

Necessity:
- D04: only source that the restriction "falls away in the context of listed schemes" and that it reduces
  the votes required for an extraordinary resolution; also states both resolution types. Without it the
  listed answer has no basis (AIMA gives the restriction for unlisted schemes only); a solver applying the
  restriction everywhere answers "Yes, yes".
- D12: only source of the extraordinary resolution's two limbs (majority of votes cast; majority of votes
  that can be cast). Without it the unlisted answer rests on recall of the statutory definition.
Delete-and-guess: D04 gone -> "Yes, yes" or "No, yes" by guess (no listed exception in the bundle). D12 gone
-> the unlisted limb from recall ("at least 50% of the votes that may be cast by members entitled to vote")
gives the gold: a recall risk to report, as for a headline figure of a public law.

After clarification: two documents; the question supplies the case, no rule.

Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: exception in one of two parallel rules (playbook); Guide levers:
scatter the pieces (the rule in Doc 13, its exception and scope in Doc 2). Two parallel asks.

Source risks: D12 recall risk above. The Voting Restriction sentence carries its own listed exception, so a
solver who reads it whole has the answer. Numbers in the question are not printed in the documents: the
reasoning must use no digits (form number check).

Author calculation: listed: 35 < 40 (votes cast), fails. Unlisted: votes that can be cast = 100 - 40 = 60;
35 > 60 / 2 = 30 and 35 > 0 against: passes. Checked by hand 2026-10-15.
