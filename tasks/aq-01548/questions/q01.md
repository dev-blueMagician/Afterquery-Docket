## Question

On the day Gennaro Mattiaccio moved for a temporary restraining order against DHA Group, what onset date for his disabling condition did his Social Security disability application, as it then stood, give?

## Answer

May 31, 2012

## Components

- c1: May 31, 2012

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 2: Mattiaccio filed his motion for a temporary restraining order on March 4, 2013. It asked the court to bar the defendants from answering a Virginia custody subpoena.

Step 2. Doc 8: he applied to the Social Security Administration for disability benefits on February 5, 2013. The application said he was unable to work because of a disabling condition on May 31, 2012, the day after his termination.

Step 3. Doc 8: on March 19, 2013, he amended the onset date of his disabling condition to May 16, 2012, the day he was placed on administrative leave.

Step 4. March 4, 2013 comes before March 19, 2013, so on the day he filed the motion his application still gave May 31, 2012.

Decoy: May 16, 2012 is the amended onset that the SSA later accepted and that the court's estoppel analysis relies on. It was not in the application until March 19, 2013.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Superseded date or version
How a careless solver fails: Uses the wrong period or date
Where the trap sits: Doc 8, the judicial estoppel discussion on pages 8 to 10. Page 8 says Mattiaccio "amended his application to state that he 'became unavailable to work because of [his] disabling condition on May 16, 2012,' rather than May 31", and page 10 adds that "Ultimately, the SSA determined that he was disabled for social security disability insurance purposes as of May 16, 2012". The whole estoppel ruling is built on that later onset date, the day he was placed on administrative leave, so it is the onset a reader meets most often and the one that looks current.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: May 16, 2012. A solver who finds the Social Security passages in Doc 8 and takes the onset date the agency finally accepted, or the one the court's analysis keeps returning to, writes May 16, 2012 without checking when that date entered the application. The same slip happens if the solver reads the amendment sentence on page 8, which carries no date.
Why the correct answer avoids it: The question asks for the application as it stood on the day the restraining-order motion was filed. Doc 2 dates that filing March 4, 2013. Doc 8 dates the amendment March 19, 2013, so on March 4 the application still gave the original onset, May 31, 2012, the day after his termination. The later date only became part of the application two weeks after the filing.
