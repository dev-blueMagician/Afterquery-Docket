## Question

Leaving out the stock-based compensation and depreciation and amortization included in them, by how much did Snap's restructuring charges for the six months ended June 30, 2026 exceed those it recorded for 2024? Answer in millions of dollars, to the nearest million.

## Answer

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h3. Neither release states how much stock-based compensation or D&A each restructuring contained; each is the gap between the expense-by-function table (footnoted "includes restructuring charges") and the reconciliation line, which leaves restructuring amounts in the restructuring add-back. The 2024 footnote's "$70.2 million of cash severance, stock-based compensation expense, and other charges" is a subtotal that still contains stock-based compensation.
Hard design (playbook section 4): two component rebuilds in two documents and a printed subtotal lure; the question names the excluded components (it must, to define the measure) but not where they sit or that they must be backed out of table gaps.
Ambiguity: (a) "restructuring charges for the six months ended June 30, 2026": D06 says three and six months are both $128.5M (none in Q1, D05 has no restructuring line). (b) "those it recorded for 2024": FY2024 72,051 (D07); no 2025 charges ("No restructuring charges were incurred during the year ended December 31, 2025"). (c) Rounded $128.5M / $72.0M vs 128,505 / 72,051: 41.67 vs 41.62, both 42 at the asked precision. (d) Using the $70.2M subtotal as the 2024 amount net of stock-based compensation -> 30 or 39 (70.2 less 9.4 = 60.8 -> 40); rejected because that subtotal "include[s] ... stock-based compensation expense". (e) Payroll tax on stock-based compensation is a separate reconciliation line, not part of restructuring.
Necessity: D06 -> 2026 charges and components (unique). D07 -> 2024 charges and components (unique; D05/D06 have no 2024 columns).
