## Question

To the nearest whole percent, what share of the yearly sum the July 2022 interim response says its 15% criminal legal aid uplifts will inject is extra fee income for barristers?

## Answer

34%

## Evidence

- D07 (Doc 5, L103): "These increases would inject an estimated additional £115m p.a. at steady state, at our projected 2024/25 volumes of cases."
- D06 (Doc 6, L164): "Additional steady state annual benefit to criminal barristers of 15% or £31m - £39m"

## Sketch notes

Origin: claude. Hook h4.

Hook/mechanism: the response's single figure is on projected 2024/25 volumes, so the matching barrister figure is
the 2024-25 column (£39m), not the impact assessment's 2019-20 best estimate (£31m).

Ambiguity:
- 31 / 115 = 27% -> mixes the 2019-20 barrister figure with the 2024/25 total (Doc 5 L103 fixes the basis).
- 31 / 95 = 33% -> a share on 2019-20 volumes; the question asks for a share of the sum the response states (£115m).
- 39 / 113.4 (sum of Doc 6's 2024-25 components) = 34.4% -> same answer at whole percent.
- Consultation-stage figure £30m (Doc 7) -> not the 2024-25 column; Doc 7's 2024-25 figure is also 39.

Necessity:
- Doc 5 (D07): £115m tied to 2024/25 volumes in one statement; Docs 6 and 7 print £115m only as the top of a range.
- Doc 6 (D06) or Doc 7 (D05): barristers' 2024-25 figure 39; both print it.

After clarification: Doc 5 plus one impact assessment; the weak point is that "£95m - £115m" in either impact
assessment already pairs 115 with the top of each range.

Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: calculate across documents; temporal alignment. Wrong routes recomputed.

Source risks: likely solved by pairing range tops; three steps only.

Author calculation: 39 / 115 = 33.9%, to the nearest whole percent 34%.
