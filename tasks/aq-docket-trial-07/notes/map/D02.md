### Agency use index (actor, use, CE component, period, thresholds, counts)

- BLS (actor: the CPI program). The Table 1 row is at line 27; the prose is at lines 45-61. The CPI prose does not name the Interview or Diary component.
  - The CPI program has three uses (line 47): expenditure weights, item sampling probabilities, and outlet point of purchase.
  - Lower-level weights (line 51):
    - CPI indexes are calculated in two stages.
    - Basic indexes are calculated "for each of the 7,776 item-area combinations"; the example is bananas in Boston.
    - First-stage weights are "usually derived from the sampling frame".
    - The text also states the pre-2018 TPOPS restriction and the August 2021 change.
  - C-CPI-U monthly weights (line 53):
    - Item estimates are summed "across 32 areas" and allocated by "each area’s relative expenditure share for the prior 12 months".
    - "The CE program delivers microdata on a quarterly basis for the monthly weights in the C-CPI-U".
    - The C-CPI-U is published first in preliminary form, then revised on a schedule.
  - Second-stage aggregation weights for CPI-U, CPI-W and R-CPI-E (line 55):
    - The New York all-items index is "the average of its 243 basic indexes".
    - The weights cover "the 243 elementary items in the 32 elementary areas".
    - The weights are "updated biennially".
  - Item sampling (line 59):
    - "approximately 70 expenditure classes divided into 243 item strata", made up of ELIs.
    - "Every year, the CPI program uses the most recent 2 years of CE data to select new item samples".
  - Outlet frame (line 61): "Since 2019" this replaces TPOPS.
  - BLS also supplies other agencies:
    - DPINR produces the SPM thresholds (line 77).
    - BLS produces special and custom tabulations for the IRS (line 91).
    - It prepares special tabulations of annual data by age for CMS (line 87).
    - It provides specially prepared data to DOD (line 83).
  - Fitness entry: "CPI (BLS): relevance, accuracy, and timeliness" (line 135).

  - SPM:
    - The Table 1 row is at line 31.
    - "The Census Bureau, in cooperation with BLS, produces the SPM".
    - The thresholds are experimental, are posted on the DPINR webpage and are sent to Census.
    - The thresholds are based on "5 years of quarterly CE Interview Survey data on out-of-pocket expenditures for food, clothing, shelter, and utilities (FCSU), by consumer units with two children". Values are converted to a reference unit of two adults and two children.
    - A multiplier is applied for other basic goods and services.
    - Census applies equivalence scales and adjusts the housing portion for geographic differences (line 77).
    - Fitness entry: "Supplemental Poverty Measure (Census): coherence, timeliness, interpretability" (line 141).

### Operative definitions, rules and scope exceptions

- Definitions:
  - "“Sponsors” are federal or state agencies on behalf of whom the Census Bureau manages surveys." Funds are appropriated to the sponsor, and the work is done on a cost-reimbursable basis under an interagency agreement (note 1, L191).
  - Nonresponse bias: "the systematic error that occurs when results collected from respondents differ in meaningful ways from those that would be collected, but by definition are not collectable, from nonrespondents" (L125). Note 58 (L305) gives examples.
  - Measurement error: "the difference between the reported value of a variable of interest and the true value of that variable" (L127).
  - Economies of scale: "achieved when increasing inputs in production by x percent increases average costs by less than x percent" (note 18, L225; the example uses a 50 percent family-size increase).
  - Spendable income (DOS): "the amount of money that households have available for spending after deductions for taxes, gifts and contributions, savings (including insurance and retirement), and U.S. shelter and household utility expenses" (L99).
  - Post allowances: "equivalent to cost-of-living allowances" (note 46, L281).
  - OCOLA: "a nontaxable allowance paid to U.S. military personnel stationed overseas to partially offset higher overseas prices on nonhousing goods and services" (L83).
  - UCC: "a special code used in the CE program to identify detailed expenditure items or categories". Bundled UCCs mean "data users cannot identify expenditures on the separate items" (note 40, L269).
  - ELIs: "the first-stage sampling units for consumer goods and services selected within each sample outlet" (L59).
  - CPI-U, C-CPI-U, CPI-W and CPI-E are described at L47. Note 11 adds that the C-CPI-U uses a superlative index formula.
  - The SPM is "an experimental poverty measure" (L77).
  - Schedule A (note 38, L265).
  - Collection Financial Standards (L91).
  - The six quality dimensions (Table 2, L105-115).
- Rules and thresholds:
  - OMB "encourages all federal survey programs to study their nonresponse bias, and OMB requires all federal surveys whose response rates are below 80 percent to conduct such a study" (L125; Guideline 3.2.9, note 59). "Both the CE Interview and Diary surveys have response rates below 80 percent and therefore are subject to the OMB requirement" (L125).
  - CONUS COLA: "more than 8 percent". The statute requires comparability with the CPI (L85).
  - OCOLA: an increase applies if overseas market-basket costs rise relative to average CONUS costs, all else held constant (L83).
  - DOS: costs "at least 3 percent above" Washington, D.C. (L99).
  - IRS sales tax: the Internal Revenue Code gives taxpayers "an option to deduct state and local general sales tax instead of state and local income tax" (L91; note 36).
  - IRS Collection Financial Standards: "Taxpayers may use the standard monthly amount, which is determined by family size" (L91).
  - SPM: "not used to determine eligibility for government programs" (L77). The thresholds are "not subject to the same review as official BLS projects" (L77).
  - CPI cadences: C-CPI-U allocation uses the prior 12 months (L53), item samples use 2 years of data (L59), and weights update biennially (L55).
  - Census: residential improvements are "benchmarked to the annual total using CE data" (L77).
- Scope exceptions:
  - The article "looks only at the secondary uses" of CE data by BLS. The analyses and reports BLS produces on the CE itself are "outside the scope" (note 3, L195).
  - The BLS section excludes CE products and publications (note 4, L197).
  - Before 2018, CE data were used only for categories with non-TPOPS outlet samples. From the August 2021 sample, CE data are used for all lower-level weights (L51).
  - The C-CPI-U is "unlike the other two official CPI data series" in being preliminary and then revised (L53).
  - The CPI-W is "based only on expenditures made by those in wage-earning or clerical occupations" (L47).
  - CMS uses CE data only for out-of-pocket spending "not covered by insurance" (L87).
  - The OCOLA and CONUS COLA cover nonhousing costs (L83, L85).
  - The sales tax option is "especially important to taxpayers residing in states with no income tax" (L91).
