## Question

Measured from the month in which seasonally adjusted Midwest nondurable goods manufacturing employment hit its lowest level between January 1990 and December 2019, what were the percentage changes to December 2019 in that employment and in seasonally adjusted U.S. rail transportation employment, in that order? Give each to one decimal place with its sign.

## Answer

+10.4% and -8.6%

## Components

- c1: +10.4%
- c2: -8.6%

## Reasoning

1. Doc 4 (chart of Midwest durable and nondurable goods employment, seasonally adjusted, in thousands, 1990 to 2019): the nondurable goods column reaches its lowest value, 1,318.5, in Mar 2010. Jan 2010 and Feb 2010 both show 1,319.3, and Dec 2019 shows 1,456.0.
2. Doc 4: 1,456.0 - 1,318.5 = 137.5 thousand.
3. Doc 4: 137.5 / 1,318.5 = +10.4% (one decimal place).
4. Doc 5 (chart of employment in rail transportation, seasonally adjusted, in thousands): Mar 2010 is 178.8 and Dec 2019 is 163.4.
5. Doc 5: 163.4 - 178.8 = -15.4 thousand.
6. Doc 5: -15.4 / 178.8 = -8.6% (one decimal place).
7. Doc 4's sentence that manufacturing "bottomed out in February 2010" describes all manufacturing, and its nondurable goods passage measures the recovery from June 2009, the business-cycle trough. Neither is the month of the nondurable series' own lowest value.
