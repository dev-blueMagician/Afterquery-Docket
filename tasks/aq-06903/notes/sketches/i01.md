## Question

Before working-capital effects, and repricing July's estimate to the gold and molybdenum prices assumed in April, how much did Freeport's 2026 operating cash flow estimate change from its April to its July 2026 release? Signed, in billions of dollars to two decimals.

## Answer

+0.19

## Evidence

- D03 (Operating Cash Flows, p10): "FCX’s consolidated operating cash flows are expected to approximate $8.7 billion for the year 2026, including $0.2 billion of working capital and other sources"
- D03 (same sentence): "assuming prices of $6.00 per pound of copper, $4,500 per ounce of gold and $25.00 per pound of molybdenum for the remainder of 2026"
- D04 (Operating Cash Flows, p9): "FCX’s consolidated operating cash flows are expected to approximate $8.3 billion for the year 2026, net of $0.3 billion of working capital and other uses"
- D04 (same sentence): "assuming prices of $6.00 per pound of copper, $4,000 per ounce of gold and $30.00 per pound of molybdenum for the second half of 2026"
- D04 (same paragraph): "$40 million for each $100 per ounce change in the average price of gold and $45 million for each $2 per pound change in the average price of molybdenum"

## Lever stack

Shape: Hard shape B (compute, then let a rule change the result) on shape 2 (an original and a revised estimate put on one basis)

- L1: Like for like across a definition change | trap: D04 "approximate $8.3 billion for the year 2026, net of $0.3 billion of working capital and other uses" | settles: D03 "approximate $8.7 billion for the year 2026, including $0.2 billion of working capital and other sources" | missed gives: -0.31
- L2: Same series, right edition | trap: D03 "$50 million for each $100 per ounce change in the average price of gold and $90 million for each $2 per pound change in the average price of molybdenum" | settles: D04 "The impact of price changes for the second half of 2026 on operating cash flows would approximate $150 million" | missed gives: +0.13
- L3: Calculate across documents | trap: D04 "$4,000 per ounce of gold and $30.00 per pound of molybdenum for the second half of 2026" | settles: D03 "$4,500 per ounce of gold and $25.00 per pound of molybdenum for the remainder of 2026" | missed gives: +0.10

Steps:

1. Put both estimates before working capital: April's $8.7 billion includes $0.2 billion of working-capital sources (8.5); July's $8.3 billion is after $0.3 billion of uses (8.6).
2. Reprice July's second half from $4,000 gold and $30 molybdenum to April's $4,500 and $25 with July's own sensitivities: +5 x $40 million - 2.5 x $45 million = +$87.5 million, so 8.6875.
3. 8.6875 - 8.5 = +0.1875, +0.19.

Decoy: -0.40 (the headline estimates, 8.3 less 8.7)

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1 (notes/hooks.md). FCX's two releases each estimate 2026 operating cash flow on a different working-capital sign and a different price deck; the headline cut of $0.4 billion becomes a rise once both are on one basis.
Hard design (playbook section 4): three levers on one path. L1 is quiet: April's figure "includ[es]" working-capital sources while July's is "net of" uses, so subtracting both cited amounts (a natural slip) gives -0.41, and ignoring them gives -0.31. L2: April's release prints its own, larger sensitivities ($50M/$90M per step, for April-December); only July's ($40M/$45M, for the second half) belong to July's estimate. L3: the April price deck is printed only in D03. The question's words ("working-capital", "repricing", "prices assumed in April") lead to the estimate sentences in both releases, which hold the traps as well as the settlements; no word points at one sensitivity sentence over the other.
Ambiguity: (a) "Before working-capital effects": April 8.7 - 0.2 = 8.5, July 8.3 + 0.3 = 8.6; subtracting both (8.5, 8.0) misreads "net of ... uses" (D04 p9) and gives -0.41. (b) Which sensitivities: the question reprices "July's estimate", and only D04's second-half sensitivities describe it; April's (D03 p10) describe April's April-December estimate (+0.13 lure). (c) Copper is $6.00 in both decks, so no copper adjustment. (d) Direction: higher gold and lower molybdenum prices respectively raise and cut cash flow (plain economics; the sensitivities are per "change in the average price"). (e) Sign convention: "change from its April to its July 2026 release" = July - April. (f) Precision: two decimals of the printed approximations; 0.1875 is not on a rounding boundary.
Necessity: D03 - April estimate ($8.7 billion), its working-capital amount and its price deck; D04 never reprints April's full-year cash-flow estimate or deck. D04 - July estimate, its working-capital amount, deck and sensitivities; D03 has none of them.
Delete-and-guess: without D03 a solver has no April estimate or deck (a guess of "unchanged deck" gives +0.10 at best); without D04 there is no July estimate. Neither guess is the gold.
