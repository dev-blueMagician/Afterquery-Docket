## Question

The submissions disagree on how much of Australia's grocery market its two largest supermarket chains now hold between them. To the nearest whole percentage point, how far apart are the highest and the lowest figure given?

## Answer

13 percentage points

## Evidence

- D04 (Poultry meat industry - structure, line 32): "Given the nearly 70% market power of the two major supermarkets"
- D04 (line 46): "with two major supermarkets controlling around 70% of food and grocery retail"
- D03 (opening, line 16): "The two predominant companies (Woolworths Group and Coles Group) have a combined share of 57% of the market."
- D05 (line 60): "accounting for about 65 per cent of the market in Australia"
- D15 (line 14): "having a combined market share of ~65%"
- D06 (line 203): "In 2022-2023, the share of products sold through Coles was 28% and Woolworths 37%"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: documents that disagree on one quantity. Five submissions put a present-day figure on the two chains' combined share: about 65% (D05), ~65% (D15), 28% + 37% = 65% (D06, not summed in the text), 57% (D03) and nearly/around 70% (D04, a chicken growers' submission, in two sentences about processors that name neither chain). Highest 70, lowest 57.
Ambiguity: (a) 8 points (65 against 57) -> misses D04, which gives a figure for "the two major supermarkets"; (b) 31 points (88 against 57) -> D06 line 203's 88% is a past peak ("during the early 2000's"), and its 26% + 32% is for 1995; the question says "now"; (c) 25 or 12 points using 82% or 70% for four companies (D15, D06, D09, D03 line 14) -> four companies, not two; (d) 15 or 18 points using D07's 55% (two liquor retailers) or D12's 75% (three chains, fresh produce) -> wrong market or wrong number of chains; (e) D14's 70% is one hardware retailer's share of plants. (f) "nearly 70%" read as a little under 70 -> the same submission also says "around 70%"; hence "To the nearest whole percentage point" in the question; the round figure the submission states is 70.
Necessity: D04 -> the highest figure, stated nowhere else (every percentage in the bundle was listed and checked); D03 -> the lowest figure, stated nowhere else. D05, D15 and D06 only sit between the two.
Delete-and-guess: without D04 the answer is 8 (65 against 57); without D03 it is 5 (70 against 65). Closed-book, the commonly reported figure is about 65%, which gives no gap. None is the gold.
After clarification: two indispensable documents; the question names no figure, no submission and neither chain.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: who belongs where (two-chain, three-chain and four-company figures; grocery, fresh produce, liquor and plants), reconcile documents that disagree. Decoys: 70% for four companies in D03 line 14, 82% for four in D15/D06/D09, 75% for three in D12, 78% for three in D09, 55% for two liquor retailers in D07, 70% for Bunnings in D14.
Step audit: nothing in the question points at D04; its figure sits twice in passages about poultry processors. D03's figure is in its second sentence. One unpointed step (finding D04's figure), reachable by a full pass over every percentage.
Source risks: every figure but D03's is hedged ("about", "~", "nearly", "around"). The gold number can also be reached by a wrong route: a reader who takes another 70% in the bundle (four companies in D03 line 14, four liquor retailers in D07, Bunnings in D14) as the two-chain high still gets 13. That costs no fairness but lowers what the question separates; a names form ("which submission gives the highest, which the lowest") would not have it and is held as a fallback.
Author calculation: 70 (D04) - 57 (D03) = 13 percentage points. Alternates: 65 - 57 = 8; 70 - 65 = 5; 88 - 57 = 31. Rechecked by script.
