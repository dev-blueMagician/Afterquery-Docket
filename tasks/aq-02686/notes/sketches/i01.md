## Question

Once EPA's 2026 significant-new-use proposal is final, a large recycler reclaims a covered oxide with 90%-efficient dust controls, exporting to non-FEOC prospects among: a Chinese city's 50%-owned Polish firm; a private Shanghai company's Korean subsidiary; a Japanese firm where a Chinese province picks two of five directors, casting 20% of board votes. What would notifying EPA cost it altogether?

## Answer

$45,708

## Evidence

- D07 (I.D.1, estimated costs for SNUN submissions): "If a SNUN is submitted, costs are an estimated $45,496 per SNUN submission for large business submitters"
- D07 (I.D.2, estimated costs for export notifications): "a one-time notice to EPA must be provided for the first export or intended export to a particular country"
- D07 (I.D.2): "the estimated cost of the export notification requirement on a per unit basis is approximately $106"
- D07 (proposed 721.12219(a)(2)(iii)): "except with the use of dust controls with an overall minimum capture and control efficiency of 99%"
- D04 (II.E, response on board seats): "DOE intends for the value of a board seat to equal the value of its voting power on the board"
- D04 (III.D, last paragraph): "subsidiaries of the FEOC are not automatically considered to be FEOCs themselves based solely on their parent being a covered nation jurisdictional entity"
- D04 (V.E): "(i) 25% or more of the entity's board seats, voting rights, or equity interest, with each metric evaluated independently, are cumulatively held by that other entity"
