## Question

Where CMS reports both enrolled-hospice counts and home health agencies sharing one address, by what percentage did those States' combined hospice count grow from 2019 to 2023? One decimal place.

## Answer

57.7%

## Evidence

- D06 (Doc 5, Table 1 title, offset 15,561): "Table 1--Number of Enrolled Hospices in CY 2019 and CY 2023"
- D06 (Doc 5, Table 1, Nevada row, offset 16,047): "Nevada.......................................................... 55 138 151"
- D06 (Doc 5, Table 1, Texas row, offset 16,123): "Texas........................................................... 718 1,081 51"
- D05 (Doc 6, section II.B, offset 18,479): "CMS has recently detected several situations in Ohio where numerous HHAs are operating out of a single, common practice location"
- D05 (Doc 6, section II.B, offset 18,838): "Similar situations have been seen in Texas, Michigan, North Carolina, and Nevada."

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. Doc 6 names five States for HHAs sharing a practice address; Doc 5 counts enrolled hospices in CY 2019 and CY 2023 for four States. Two States meet both, Nevada and Texas. Their combined count went from 773 to 1,219, a figure no document prints, beside the table's own per-State "Percentage increase" column.
Ambiguity: (1) California: Doc 5, right under Table 1 (16,467), says Los Angeles County hospices operated "out of a single building". That is about hospices; the question says home health agencies, and Doc 6 names California only for Los Angeles County's growth in HHA numbers (17,400-18,400) and for "non-California" counts. (2) Ohio, Michigan, North Carolina: named in Doc 6's cluster passage but have no CY 2019 / CY 2023 hospice counts anywhere (Ohio and Georgia appear in Doc 5 only in prose; Georgia's counts are for CY 2019 and CY 2025), so the first condition excludes them. (3) Growth of the "combined hospice count" is the growth of the summed count; an average (101.0) or sum (202) of the printed per-State percentages is not the growth of a combined count. (4) Nevada's later 188 is for CY 2025, outside the named years. Searched: every "single", "same practice location", "co-located", "one location", "single site" in all seven documents; every Nevada, North Carolina, Arizona, California, Texas, Michigan, Ohio, Georgia mention in Docs 3, 5, 6.
Necessity: Doc 6 -> which States have HHAs sharing an address; Nevada and North Carolina are named once each in the whole bundle for this (18,892-18,912); Doc 5 and Doc 3 never describe HHAs sharing an address -> without Doc 6 the two-State set is unknowable and the answer could be any subset's growth (51, 151, 126, 98.5, 99.0 ...). Doc 5 -> the four States' counts; Doc 6 and Doc 3 print no hospice counts -> without Doc 5 there is nothing to add or divide.
After clarification: the wording names neither State and no count; both documents still needed.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: who belongs where (a filtered population, so decoys apply) and a calculated figure beside printed look-alikes. Decoys recomputed: California with Nevada and Texas 1,903 -> 3,778 = 98.5; all four States 2,055 -> 4,089 = 99.0; Texas alone (Doc 6's later partial mention, "co-located HHAs ... such as Michigan and Texas", 36,507) 50.6; average of the printed 151 and 51 = 101.0.
Source risks: Doc 6's later passage names only Michigan and Texas for co-located HHAs; it does not contradict the earlier sentence that adds North Carolina and Nevada. The population takes two conditions to state, so the question runs to 30 words.
Author calculation: Doc 5 Table 1 Nevada 55 (CY 2019), 138 (CY 2023); Texas 718, 1,081. 55 + 718 = 773; 138 + 1,081 = 1,219; 1,219 - 773 = 446; 446 / 773 = 0.57697 -> 57.7% (one decimal). Script check 2026-10-11: 57.697.
