## Question

The autoimmune subtype study and the brain spatial-transcriptomics study each kept only the top-ranked genes by a variability measure in at least one analysis. How many percentage points larger was the autoimmune study's kept share of all its measured genes than the brain study's, to one decimal place?

## Answer

22.1 percentage points

## Evidence

- D02 (Sec. 5.1, text line 431): "raw expression measurements available for 9,671 genes"
- D02 (Appendix A, text line 723): "the top 3000 genes ranked by MAD were retained"
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "3,592 in-tissue Visium spots and 33,538 gene features"
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "selecting the top 3,000 HVGs"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h2. Autoimmune: 3000 / 9,671 = 31.0206%. Brain HVG analysis: 3,000 / 33,538 =
8.9451%. Difference 22.0755 -> 22.1 percentage points (rounded shares 31.0 - 8.9 = 22.1 agree).
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: bury one item late (the 3000 is only in Appendix A, the
last lines of the paper); calculate across documents; state the rounding. Decoy: the main text's
"979 highly variable genes" (line 439) and Table 4 "MAD-based model ... 979" (line 585) are the set
left after small modules were removed; 979 / 9,671 = 10.1230% gives 1.2 percentage points.
Source risks: Sec. 5.5 calls the 979 "the same set of pre-selected genes" (line 577); the wording
must point at the ranking cut, not the final modelled set, or 979 becomes defensible.
Author calculation check and counting convention: percentages of all measured genes, one decimal.
