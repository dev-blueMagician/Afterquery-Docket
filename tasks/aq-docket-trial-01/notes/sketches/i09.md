## Question

How many more observations did the brain spatial-factor study's model have in its main tissue analysis than the autoimmune subtype study's mixture factor model had?

## Answer

13,853 observations

## Evidence

- D05 (Sec. 1.1, text line 29): "we will use the term ‘observations’ for the rows of Y and spatial locations for the columns"
- D05 (Sec. 1.1, text line 29): "‘observation’ may refer to a spatial location together with its vector of measurements across all genes / features; we do not use this terminology here"
- D05 (Sec. 1.1, text line 31): "In the spatial transcriptomics analysis, each gene, corresponding to a row of Y"
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "The low-expression filtering retains 14,638 genes."
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "We treat the filtered analysis as our main analysis"
- D02 (Sec. 4.1, text line 175): "The posterior probability that observation i belongs to cluster k"
- D02 (Sec. 5.1, text line 431): "It contains a total of 785 samples"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: i05 without the "as each paper defines them" signpost. The spatial paper defines
its observations as the rows of Y, which are genes in the brain case study, and explicitly sets
aside the usual spatial-transcriptomics sense (a spot). Main analysis: 14,638 genes. The
autoimmune model's observations are its 785 samples ("observation i" in the E-step). 14,638 - 785.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: entity resolution (a term the paper defines against common
usage); calculate across documents. Decoy carrying the asked label in ordinary usage: spots, 3,592
- 785 = 2,807 (or 3,192 training spots - 785 = 2,407); full-gene 32,753; HVG 2,215.
Source risks: without the signpost, a platform model check could call "observations" ambiguous;
the spatial paper's explicit definition and disclaimer are the defence. Removal: 14,638 and 785 are
paper-specific counts, not guessable.
Author calculation check and counting convention: 14,638 - 785 = 13,853.
