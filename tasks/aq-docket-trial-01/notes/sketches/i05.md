## Question

Counting observations as each paper defines them, how many more observations went into the brain spatial-factor study's main tissue analysis than into the autoimmune study's mixture factor model?

## Answer

13,853 observations

## Evidence

- D05 (Sec. 1.1, text line 29): "we will use the term ‘observations’ for the rows of Y and spatial locations for the columns"
- D05 (Sec. 2/1.1, text line 31): "In the spatial transcriptomics analysis, each gene, corresponding to a row of Y"
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "The low-expression filtering retains 14,638 genes."
- D05 (Sec. 6, text line 783): "We treat the filtered analysis as our main analysis"
- D02 (Sec. 4.1, text line 175): "The posterior probability that observation i belongs to cluster k"
- D02 (Sec. 5.1, text line 431): "It contains a total of 785 samples"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: definition applied across documents (entity resolution). The spatial-factor paper
calls the rows of its data matrix "observations"; in the brain case study the rows are genes, and it
explicitly declines the usual spatial-transcriptomics sense (a spot). Main analysis: 14,638 genes.
The autoimmune model's observations are its 785 samples. 14,638 - 785 = 13,853.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: entity resolution (who belongs where); calculate across
documents. Decoys: spots as observations, 3,592 - 785 = 2,807; full-gene 33,538 - 785 = 32,753;
HVG 3,000 - 785 = 2,215; autoimmune genes 979 instead of samples gives 13,659.
Source risks: "as each paper defines them" is needed for one defensible answer and signposts the
definition; D02 never defines "observation" formally (E-step wording only).
Author calculation check and counting convention: 14,638 - 785 = 13,853.
