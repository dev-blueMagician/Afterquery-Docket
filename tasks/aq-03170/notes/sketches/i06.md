## Question

Two of these letters give the same Federal Register citation different dates. How many days separate those two dates?

## Answer

6 days

## Evidence

- D01 (L113, footnote 14): "68 Fed. Reg. 56,692 (Sept. 25, 2003)"
- D04 (L35, footnote 7): "68 Fed. Reg. 56,692 (SEC, Adopting Release, Oct. 1, 2003)"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h10. Blackstone (Doc 1, footnote 14) and FIA (Doc 9, footnote 7) both cite "Custody of Funds or
Securities of Clients by Investment Advisers" at 68 Fed. Reg. 56,692. Blackstone dates it Sept. 25, 2003, FIA Oct. 1,
2003. Guide shape: two documents disagree on one item they both cover; find the overlap, then the gap.
Ambiguity: 22 days (the proposing release, Feb. 15, 2023 against Mar. 9, 2023) -> fails "the same Federal Register
citation": the February date is attached to the release number (D01 L75 and L90, D03 L20, D12 L9), and every dated
cite of 88 Fed. Reg. 14672 says March 9, 2023 (D09 L22, D10 L21, D04 L18, D03 L20, D12 L25, D13 L49). 7 days (both end
days counted) -> "separate". Other citations two letters share carry one date each: 88 Fed. Reg. 63206, Sept. 14, 2023
(D01 L130-131, D09 L47, D10 L24); 88 Fed. Reg. 59818, Aug. 30, 2023 (D09 L21-22, D10 L20). Searched: every "Fed. Reg."
and "FR" cite in the thirteen letters (D01, D03, D04, D09, D10, D12, D13; the other six have none).
Necessity: Doc 1 -> Sept. 25, 2003 (nowhere else); Doc 9 -> Oct. 1, 2003 (nowhere else).
Delete-and-guess: without Doc 1 -> no citation carries two dates; a solver says there is no such pair or gives the
proposing release's 22 days. From recall it would have to know this release's adoption and publication dates and
guess that this is the pair. Without Doc 9 -> the same. With no documents -> nothing to go on. Not the gold either way.
After clarification: two documents; the question supplies no release, page, year or letter.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Who belongs where (which citation appears in which
document). Decoy with its own value: the proposing release's two dates, 22 days.
Unpointed steps: the shared citation sits in two footnotes of two letters and no word in the question names the
release, the year or the letters; the lure (22 days) sits in a third place. One search, one exclusion, one subtraction.
Source risks: a footnote detail. The Guide warns that an obscure detail is not reasoning, and the form has an "about
the set" advisory; a reviewer may call this trivia. Both dates are correct in the world (adopted, then published); the
question asserts only that the letters print different dates for one citation.
Author calculation: Sept. 25, 2003 -> Oct. 1, 2003: 5 days to Sept. 30, plus 1 = 6 days (script: date(2003, 10, 1) -
date(2003, 9, 25) = 6 days).
