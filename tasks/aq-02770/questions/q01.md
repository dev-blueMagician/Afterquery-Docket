## Question

For a gas-fired commercial warm air furnace built at the federal minimum thermal efficiency on the day DOE held its public webinar on the proposal to create TE2, how low could TE2 be by DOE's estimates if part load were weighted at the largest share of operating time NEEA's modeling gave it? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

76.0 percent

## Components

- c1: 76.0 percent

## Reasoning

Step 1. Source input (Doc 4, Background): DOE proposed the TE2 metric in a notice of proposed rulemaking published in February 2022, and it held a webinar public meeting on that proposal on March 29, 2022.

Step 2. Source input (Doc 2, Background): gas-fired commercial warm air furnaces manufactured before January 1, 2023 must have a thermal efficiency (TE) of not less than 80 percent, and the 81 percent minimum applies only to furnaces manufactured starting on January 1, 2023. The webinar of Step 1 came before that date, so a furnace built that day at the federal minimum has a TE of 80 percent.

Step 3. Source input (Doc 4, Discussion and section II.C): DOE estimated that a furnace with a TE of 81 percent could show a TE2 as low as 77.5 percent, the case of high jacket losses and poor part-load performance. Jacket losses could account for as much as a 2.5 percent difference in efficiency. The estimate rests on the 50 percent weighting of part load that DOE adopted.

Step 4. Calculation (Doc 4): 81 - 77.5 - 2.5 = 1 percentage point, the part of that shortfall due to part-load performance at DOE's weighting. The rest is jacket loss, heat escaping through the cabinet, which does not depend on how part load is weighted.

Step 5. Source input (Doc 4, section II.C): in NEEA's modeling a furnace ran at full load as little as 25 percent of the time and at part load 75 percent of the time, the largest part-load share.

Step 6. Calculation (Doc 4): 75 / 50 = 1.5 percentage points once the part-load shortfall of Step 4 is scaled from DOE's weighting to NEEA's largest part-load share.

Step 7. Calculation (Doc 2 and Doc 4): 80 - 2.5 - 1.5 = 76.0 percent, the lowest TE2 for that furnace: its TE from Step 2 less the jacket losses of Step 3 and the re-weighted part-load shortfall of Step 6.
