## Question

For a gas-fired commercial warm air furnace built at the federal minimum thermal efficiency on the day DOE held its public webinar on the proposal to create TE2, how low could TE2 be by DOE's estimates if part load were weighted at the largest share of operating time NEEA's modeling gave it? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

76.0 percent

## Evidence

- D04 (I.C Background, l.103): "The February 2022 NOPR had a 60-day comment period and DOE held a webinar public meeting on March 29, 2022."
- D02 (II.B Background, l.135): "for gas-fired CWAFs manufactured starting on January 1, 1994, until January 1, 2023, TE at the maximum rated capacity (i.e., rated maximum input) must be not less than 80 percent"
- D04 (II Discussion, l.143): "have an actual range of efficiencies from 77.5 to 82 percent using the appendix B test procedure for TE2"
- D04 (II.C, l.189): "could account for as much as a 2.5 percent difference in efficiency"
- D04 (II.C, l.187): "adopted the 50 percent weighting at full load and part load"
- D04 (II.C, l.187): "to operating as little as 25 percent of the time at full load (and 75 percent at part load)"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. DOE's printed example is an 81 percent TE furnace (77.5 to 82). A gas-fired furnace built in 2022 was held to 80, a date rule only D02 states. The 3.5-point shortfall splits into jacket loss (2.5, no weighting) and part load (1 at DOE's 50 percent weighting); only the part-load piece moves when the weight goes to NEEA's largest modeled part-load share, 75 percent, a figure only D04 prints.
Ambiguity: 77.5 (printed) -> fails "built in 2022": 81 applies from January 1, 2023 (D02 l.136). 76.5 (80 - 3.5) -> fails "if part load were weighted at the largest share ... NEEA's modeling gave it": that keeps the 50 percent weighting (D04 l.187). 76.9 or 76.1 (30 or 70 percent) -> "largest share" of part load is 75 (D04 l.187: 30 and 75 are the part-load shares, 70 and 25 the full-load ones). 76.3 (2 percent read as relative) -> the documents treat these percentages as points: 81 less 3.5 is 77.5 (D04 l.143; D03 l.164-168 turns 2 percent into 1 percent at half weight). Exact jacket figure 0.75 x 3.3 = 2.475 (D03 l.158) gives 76.025, the same answer at one decimal.
Necessity: D02 -> the 80 percent minimum for a 2022-built gas-fired unit (compliance date January 1, 2023) -> D03/D04 print "from 80 to 81 percent" with no date, D05/D06/D07 nothing, D01 consumer AFUE classes only -> without D02 a solver cannot tell which level applied in 2022 (public regulation: recall is the open risk, to be probed). D04 -> NEEA's 75 percent part-load share, and the 2.5 / 77.5 / 50 percent figures -> searched "NEEA", "75 percent", "part load" in all seven: D03 has the estimates but no modeled shares, D02 has NEEA comments on other subjects -> without D04 the re-weighting has no value. D03 is not required: D04 alone yields the part-load piece (3.5 - 2.5 = 1 at 50 percent). D03 states the same pieces directly and is left unticked.
After clarification: D02 and D04 both still needed; the question prints neither 80, 75, 2.5 nor 50.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Same series, right edition (the standard in force for the manufacture year). Not a which-items question. Wrong-route values recomputed in scratch calc_te2.py: 77.0 (81 base), 76.5 (weighting unchanged), 74.8 (whole 3.5 scaled), 78.5 (jacket left out), 76.9 / 76.1 / 77.0 (30, 70, 25 percent). None equals 76.0.
Source risks: the re-weighting is a what-if DOE did not compute; D04 ties NEEA's shares to the 50 percent weighting decision and D03 shows the weighting arithmetic, so the operands are connected in the documents. D03/D04 figures sit past character 26,000 of their documents: the form's reasoning number check will show false alarms for 77.5, 2.5, 50 and 75.
Author calculation: printed inputs 80 (D02), 77.5 for an 81 unit, 2.5, 50, 75 (D04) -> 81 - 77.5 = 3.5; 3.5 - 2.5 = 1.0 (part load at 50 percent); 1.0 x 75 / 50 = 1.5; 80 - 2.5 - 1.5 = 76.0 percent. Exact-jacket check 80 - 2.475 - 1.5 = 76.025 -> 76.0. Script: Decimal arithmetic, half-up to one decimal.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: mechanical screen eligible (2026-10-10, rev 0 wording "built in 2022"). That wording's removal probe without D02 reached the gold on a forced guess by recall (p 0.65), so the question above anchors the date on D02's publication day instead; "built in 2022" in the notes below is the rev 0 wording.
