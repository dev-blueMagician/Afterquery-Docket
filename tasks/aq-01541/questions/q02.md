## Question

Suppose every member of the class in Trans Union's preliminarily approved settlement filed a valid claim. Taking the court's figure for the money set aside for valid-claim payments, how many dollars of it would go to each claimant who gets no automatic payment? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

$29.1

## Components

- c1: $29.1

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 7: members of the No Bankruptcy Group get an automatic payment of $100.00, and members of the Aged Bankruptcy Group do not, so the claimants who get no automatic payment are the Aged Bankruptcy Group. Approximately $2,882,000.00 is reserved for valid-claim payments. A valid claim earns a No Bankruptcy Group member three pro rata points and an Aged Bankruptcy Group member one, and each claimant's payment is its points divided by all claimants' points, so an Aged Bankruptcy Group claimant receives the value of one point.

Step 2. Doc 7: both groups are defined by Jaffe's April 22, 2025 Supplemental Report: no locatable bankruptcy record with the same nine-digit SSN, or only one filed more than ten years before the report.

Step 3. Doc 6: Jaffe's April 22, 2025 report re-ran Step Two through Thomson Reuters, which eliminated 920 individuals and left a class list of 57,233. On that list, 20,891 class members had no bankruptcy record located in PACER or Thomson Reuters, and 36,317 had only a bankruptcy record filed more than ten years before their report.

Step 4. Doc 6 and Doc 7: No Bankruptcy Group points if every member claims: 3 × 20,891 = 62,673.

Step 5. Doc 6 and Doc 7: all points: 62,673 + 36,317 = 98,990.

Step 6. Doc 6 and Doc 7: one point's share of the valid-claims money: 2,882,000 / 98,990 = 29.1 dollars (to one decimal place).

Decoys: Doc 7's "approximately $350" for Aged Bankruptcy Group claimants is the plaintiff's estimate at typical claim rates, not the payment when every member claims. Doc 6 first prints Jaffe's March 2025 split, 22,212 SSNs with no bankruptcy record and 35,941 with only older filings, which the April report superseded.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Look-alike measure
How a careless solver fails: Misapplies a rule or premise
Where the trap sits: Doc 7, Payments to Class Members (page 6): "Class members in the Aged Bankruptcy Group who submit valid claims are expected to receive approximately $350." The same estimate is repeated in the court's equitable-treatment discussion (page 17). It is a printed dollar figure for exactly the people the question asks about, the claimants who get no automatic payment. It belongs to the plaintiff's projections in that paragraph: the companion figure for the other group is described as based "on estimates derived from similar litigation as well as from typical claims rates data for similar cases", and the court calls both "these estimates based on historical data", so they assume a settlement in which only some members claim.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: $350. A solver who finds the group and its printed expected payment answers with that figure, ignoring the question's premise that every member of the class files a valid claim, and so never computes the point value at all.
Why the correct answer avoids it: The question fixes the claim rate: every member of both groups files a valid claim. Under Doc 7's formula each claimant's share is its points over all claimants' points, so with every member claiming the points are 3 × 20,891 + 36,317 = 98,990 (counts from Doc 6's April list), and one point, which is all an Aged Bankruptcy Group claimant holds, is worth 2,882,000 / 98,990 = $29.11, or $29.1. The $350 estimate rests on far fewer claimants sharing the same money, so it cannot apply.

### Failure mode 2
Trap: Superseded date or version
How a careless solver fails: Uses the wrong period or date
Where the trap sits: Doc 6, the Post-Certification Discovery section (pages 3-4): "This search resulted in 22,212 SSNs that had no bankruptcy records returned, and 35,941 SSNs that had one or more bankruptcy filings that predated the applicable consumer report by more than ten years." These are the results of Jaffe's March 12, 2025 report, a PACER search, and they paraphrase the two definitions Doc 7 gives the settlement's groups. They are the first split of the whole class list that Doc 6 prints.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: $28.1. Taking 22,212 as the No Bankruptcy Group and 35,941 as the Aged Bankruptcy Group gives 3 × 22,212 + 35,941 = 102,577 points, and 2,882,000 / 102,577 = $28.10, or $28.1.
Why the correct answer avoids it: Doc 7's footnotes 2 and 3 define both groups by Jaffe's April 22, 2025 Supplemental Report. Doc 6 says that report re-ran the search through Thomson Reuters, eliminated 920 individuals and left a class list of 57,233, and it later gives the April split, 20,891 and 36,317, where Trans Union's experts drew their samples. Those counts give 98,990 points and $29.1 per point. The March figures also sum to 58,153, not the 57,233 on the list.
