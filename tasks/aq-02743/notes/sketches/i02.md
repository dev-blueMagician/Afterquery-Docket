## Question

A town of fewer than 50,000 people with more than 15 employees takes HHS funds. Just before HHS's interim rule took effect, how many days apart were its DOJ and HHS web-accessibility deadlines?

## Answer

716 days

## Evidence

- D03 (SUMMARY, L25): "The compliance date for public entities with a total population of less than 50,000, or any special district government, is extended from April 26, 2027, to April 26, 2028."
- D03 (DATES, L28): "Effective date: This IFR is effective April 20, 2026."
- D04 (SUMMARY, L26): "The compliance date for recipients with fifteen (15) or more employees is extended from May 11, 2026, to May 11, 2027."
- D04 (DATES, L30): "Effective date: This IFR is effective May 7, 2026."

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h2. The two extensions took effect 17 days apart (DOJ April 20, 2026; HHS May 7, 2026). In between, a
body in DOJ's later tier and HHS's earlier tier had a DOJ deadline already moved to April 26, 2028 and an HHS deadline
still at May 11, 2026. Four independent steps, one per cell: DOJ tier (population), DOJ version in force (extended), HHS
tier (employees), HHS version in force (original). Neither document states the interim gap.
Ambiguity: 350 days (both original dates: May 11, 2026 to April 26, 2027) -> fails "just before HHS's interim rule took
effect": DOJ's rule was effective April 20, 2026 (D03 L28), before May 7, 2026 (D04 L30). 351 days (both extended) ->
fails the same phrase: HHS's extension was not yet in effect. 352 (HHS later tier, May 10, 2027) -> fails "more than 15
employees". 14 / 15 / 17 (same-tier pairings) -> fail the stated population or staff size. Publication date May 11,
2026 instead of the effective date May 7 -> same answer (both follow April 20).
Necessity: D03 -> April 26, 2028 for a body under 50,000 people, and the April 20, 2026 effective date -> D04 gives
"April 20, 2026" as the publication date of DOJ's rule but no DOJ compliance date -> without D03 the DOJ deadline is
unknown (a one-year shift of the public 2024 date happens to give April 26, 2028: stated recall risk). D04 -> May 11,
2026 as the HHS date still in force and May 7, 2026 as the moment asked about -> D03 never mentions HHS -> without D04
neither the moment nor the HHS deadline exists.
After clarification: both documents still needed. The wording prints both thresholds (the tier steps are meant to be
plain), no date and no version.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Same series, right edition (which version of each rule was in force);
Calculate across documents. Wrong routes recomputed (script, 2026-10-10): 350, 351, 352, 14, 15, 17.
Source risks: (1) the DOJ small-tier date equals the public 2024 date plus exactly one year, so D03's necessity rests
on its effective date and the extension itself; (2) "took effect" could be read as publication (May 11, 2026): same
answer.
Author calculation: Source inputs: Doc 4 May 11, 2026 (original date, fifteen or more employees); Doc 3 April 26, 2028
(extended date, under 50,000 people). Calculation: May 11, 2026 to May 11, 2027 = 365 days; to April 26, 2028 = 365 +
351 = 716 days elapsed. Python datetime: (2028-04-26) - (2026-05-11) = 716.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: dropped 2026-10-10, replaced by i06. Author finding: DOJ's small-tier date
(April 26, 2028) is the public 2024 date plus exactly one year, and D04 says DOJ delayed "by one year" after April 20,
2026, so D04 plus memory of the 2024 rule reaches 716 without D03. i06 uses DOJ's larger tier, whose new date
(April 26, 2027) is 367 days after the old one and cannot be rebuilt that way.
