## Question

For a public entity that also takes HHS funds, at most how many days after its extended DOJ web-accessibility deadline can its extended HHS one fall?

## Answer

380 days

## Evidence

- D03 (SUMMARY, L25): "The compliance date for State and local government entities with a total population of 50,000 or more is extended from April 24, 2026, to April 26, 2027."
- D03 (SUMMARY, L25): "The compliance date for public entities with a total population of less than 50,000, or any special district government, is extended from April 26, 2027, to April 26, 2028."
- D04 (SUMMARY, L26): "The compliance date for recipients with fifteen (15) or more employees is extended from May 11, 2026, to May 11, 2027."
- D04 (SUMMARY, L27): "(15) employees is extended from May 10, 2027, to May 10, 2028."
- D04 (background, L114): "differentiates between small and large covered entities based on population size"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. DOJ tiers its title II deadline by total population (and special-district status); HHS tiers its
section 504 deadline by number of employees. The tests are independent, so one body can sit in DOJ's earlier tier and
HHS's later tier: April 26, 2027 to May 10, 2028. Neither document states this consequence; both label their tiers
"large" and "small", which invites pairing large with large (15 days) and small with small (14 days).
Ambiguity: 15 days (large/large) and 14 days (small/small) -> fail "at most ... can": a body of 50,000 or more people
with fewer than fifteen employees is in DOJ's first tier (D03 L25) and HHS's second (D04 L26-27). 351 days (DOJ's later
tier with HHS's earlier one) -> fails "after its ... DOJ deadline": there the HHS date comes first. 381 (inclusive
count) or 379 (leap day dropped) -> "days after" is the elapsed count, and February 29, 2028 lies inside the span.
Original dates (381, April 24, 2026 to May 10, 2027) -> fail "extended".
Necessity: D03 -> DOJ's extended date April 26, 2027 and its population test -> D04 says only that DOJ delayed "by one
year" and tiers "based on population size" (no DOJ date; grep "April 26", "April 24": D04 0 hits) -> without D03 a
one-year shift of the public 2024 date gives April 24, 2027 and 382 days. D04 -> HHS's extended date May 10, 2028 and
its employee test -> D03 never mentions HHS or section 504 (grep "May 10", "May 11", "fifteen", "504": D03 0 hits) ->
without D04 there is no HHS deadline. D01, D02, D05, D06, D07 print none of the four dates.
After clarification: both documents still needed; the wording prints no date, threshold or tier test.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Who belongs where (which size test governs which
deadline). Wrong routes recomputed (script, 2026-10-10): 15, 14, 351, 381 (original dates), 382 (assumed one-year shift).
Source risks: (1) recall: a solver that remembers DOJ's April 2026 extension could supply April 26, 2027 without D03
(Document-check risk, unmeasured). (2) The gold needs the combination "50,000 or more people, fewer than fifteen
employees"; the rules allow it, no document gives an example. (3) The form's two-solver check may land on 15.
Author calculation: Source inputs: Doc 3 April 26, 2027 (first tier); Doc 4 May 10, 2028 (second tier). Calculation:
April 26, 2027 to April 26, 2028 = 366 days (February 29, 2028 inside); to May 10, 2028 = 366 + 14 = 380 days elapsed.
Python datetime: (2028-05-10) - (2027-04-26) = 380.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: held, not offered (2026-10-10). Author finding after the sketch: the gold
is also what a solver gets by pairing the earliest DOJ date with the latest HHS date under "at most", without checking
that the two size tests are independent, so the insight the design rests on is not needed to answer.
