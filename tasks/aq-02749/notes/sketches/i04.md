## Question

Under EPA's five proposed State coal-ash program approvals, how many of the 2024 Legacy Rule's all-unit amendments would lack an approved State version in exactly two States?

## Answer

10 amendments

## Evidence

- D02 (Wyoming Partial Program, L282): "1. Amendments made in the Legacy CCR Surface Impoundments and CCR Management Units Final Rule."
- D02 (Wyoming Partial Program, L280): "Wyoming is not seeking approval for the following four requirements of the Federal CCR regulations"
- D05 (Alabama Partial Program, L308): "Therefore, EPA is approving the State's version of these requirements:"
- D05 (Alabama Partial Program, L340): "2. ``Infiltration''; this additional definition in 40 CFR 257.53 is consistent with the term's plain meaning and dictionary definitions"
- D06 (Wisconsin Partial Program, L309): "Therefore, EPA is approving the State's version of these requirements:"
- D06 (Wisconsin Partial Program, L314): "5. ``Infiltration''; this additional definition in 40 CFR 257.53 is consistent with the term's plain meaning and dictionary definitions"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h32, asked from the other side so that Wyoming counts every time. Wyoming lacks an approved version of every Legacy Rule amendment (one grouped item, D02 L282, which only the later proposals itemise). Alabama lacks three more, Wisconsin nine, Virginia and Louisiana none of the twenty. An amendment is missing in exactly two States when it is missing in Wyoming and in just one of Alabama and Wisconsin.
Ambiguity: 1 forgets Wyoming, or treats it as approving everything: only "Contains both CCR and liquids" is then missing in exactly two (Alabama, Wisconsin). 9 counts the amendments missing in Wyoming alone. 8 or 2 takes one side of the Alabama/Wisconsin difference. The twelve "more prescriptive" amendments and the legacy-impoundment and management-unit provisions are missing in all five States, not two. "lack an approved State version" follows EPA's own split between "approving the State's version" and "not being approved"; an entry absent from a State's approved list has no approved version there.
Necessity: D02 -> Wyoming seeks no Legacy Rule amendment (without it the answer is 1). D05 -> Alabama's seventeen approved and three unapproved entries. D06 -> Wisconsin's eleven. D03 and D04 lack nothing on the list, so removing either leaves 10; they are not ticked.
After clarification: the wording names the rule and a count of States, nothing else.
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Who belongs where; Match, then exclude; Scatter the pieces (Wyoming's grouped item is itemised only in the other proposals). Wrong routes 1, 9, 8, 2 recomputed.
Source risks: sibling of i03 with the same gold; at most one is written up. "lack ... in exactly two" is a negative count and must stay easy to parse. Small-integer answer.
Author calculation: missing in Wyoming and Wisconsin only: 17 - 9 = 8; missing in Wyoming and Alabama only: 11 - 9 = 2; 8 + 2 = 10. Python 2026-10-10.
Screen decision/actual preliminary feedback: sketch; quick test first, write-up only on survival.
