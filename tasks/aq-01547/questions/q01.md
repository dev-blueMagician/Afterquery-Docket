## Question

Which whistleblowers in cases federal courts ruled on from July through September 2026, if any, went to federal authorities with a complaint or report while still working for an employer they later sued? Give their surnames in any order.

## Answer

Walden-Bing and Wonneman

## Components

- c1: Walden-Bing
- c2: Wonneman

## Reasoning

Step 1. Doc 1: Amy C. Walden-Bing "was employed by Horne as a Quality Assurance/Quality Control Specialist" on the Texas Rent Relief program. While she was raising her concerns with management and human resources, "Meanwhile, Walden-Bing made reports to the Office of Inspector General, the Securities and Exchange Commission, and the U.S. Department of Labor." Only after that does the ruling say she "was terminated around September 2, 2022", and her other inspector-general contact came "Two months later". So she went to federal authorities while still employed by Horne, which she sued in the qui tam filed May 8, 2023. She qualifies.

Step 2. Doc 5: Matthew David Wonneman "was employed by defendant from July 16, 2017, to May 13, 2025". He submitted Whistleblower Retaliation Complaints with the Office of the Inspector General "on or about April 15, 2025 individually, and on June 4, 2025, through [counsel]", and the ruling confirms the OIG was that of the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services. May 13, 2025 - April 15, 2025 = 28 days, so his first complaint came while he was still employed by Global Grab Technologies, which he sued on April 14, 2026. He qualifies.

Step 3. The whistleblowers who went to federal authorities with a complaint or report while still working for an employer they later sued are Walden-Bing and Wonneman.

Decoy: Doc 2's relator Claudio De Simone disclosed the alleged fraud "to federal authorities" (the FTC from November 7, 2016, then HHS and DOJ), but he "resigned from VSL" in November 2014, so none of his disclosures came while he was still working for a company he later sued.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Out-of-range period
How a careless solver fails: Wrong members in a list
Where the trap sits: Doc 2, the De Simone v. VSL Pharmaceuticals memorandum opinion, in its section "Professor De Simone's Disclosures To The Government": "Professor De Simone alleges that he repeatedly disclosed the fraud scheme alleged in the complaint to federal authorities." It lists his November 7, 2016 contact with the FTC's Chief of Staff for Advertising Practices, his December 1, 2016 letter to the FTC, more materials in February and May 2017, and letters to HHS and DOJ in July 2020 and August 2021. No other whistleblower in these rulings has a passage that sets out federal disclosures so prominently, and it uses the question's own words, "federal authorities".
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: "De Simone, Walden-Bing and Wonneman". A solver who matches "took their concerns to federal authorities" to that section adds De Simone to the two correct names without checking when, relative to his time at VSL, those disclosures were made.
Why the correct answer avoids it: Two pages earlier, in "The De Simone Formulation And VSL#3", Doc 2 says "In November 2014, Professor De Simone resigned from VSL". His first federal contact came on November 7, 2016, about two years after he left, and every later disclosure is later still. None was made while he was still working for VSL or any other employer he later sued, so he fails the question's condition, while Walden-Bing (reports before her September 2, 2022 termination, Doc 1) and Wonneman (April 15, 2025 complaint, before his May 13, 2025 departure, Doc 5) meet it.
