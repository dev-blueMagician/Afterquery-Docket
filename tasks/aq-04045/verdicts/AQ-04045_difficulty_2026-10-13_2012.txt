Full answer

For fiscal year 2024, the FCC used a 60/40 split of space‑station regulatory fees between geostationary orbit (GSO) and non‑geostationary orbit (NGSO) systems.

From the FY 2024 fee schedule shown in the orders:

- NGSO “Other” fee: $964,200 per unit × 11 units = $10,606,200
- NGSO “Less Complex” fee: $441,925 per unit × 6 units = $2,651,550
- Small‑satellite fee: $12,215 per unit × 16 units = $195,440

These NGSO amounts (including the small‑satellite deduction from the NGSO share) total about $13.45 million, which represents the 40% NGSO share. Solving for the full space‑station pool and taking the 60% GSO portion gives:

\[ \text{GSO total} \approx 0.60 \times \left(\frac{13.45\ \text{million}}{0.40}\right) \approx \$20.2\ \text{million} \]

So the FCC assessed about $20.2 million in regulatory fees on geostationary space stations as a group for FY 2024.

Let N_LC = 6 units, fee_LC = $441,925 => total Less Complex fees = 6 * 441,925 = $2,651,550.
Total NGSO fees (excluding small satellites) = 10,606,200 + 2,651,550 = $13,257,750.
