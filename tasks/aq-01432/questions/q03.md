## Question

Which paragraphs of Schedule 20 to the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 set out the banned practices that the UK's brief business guide on unfair trading lists under misleading promotional claims? Give the paragraph numbers in ascending order.

## Answer

## Reasoning

Step 1 (Doc 11): The short guide says "The banned practices fall into the following themes." Under "Misleading promotional claims" it names: bait advertising; bait and switch; pretending that a special offer will finish soon; claiming to be about to cease trading or move premises; saying a product is free when it isn't; telling someone they have won a prize when that is not true; pretending that consumers' statutory rights are a distinctive part of the offer.

Step 2 (Doc 9): The full guidance numbers each banned practice as in Schedule 20 ("paragraph 13 of Schedule 20"). The guide's behaviours match: an invitation to purchase at a price the trader has reasonable grounds to believe it cannot supply, without disclosing that (banned practice 5, bait advertising); an invitation to purchase followed by refusing to show, take orders for or deliver the item "with the intention of promoting a different product" (banned practice 6, bait and switch); falsely stating that a product will only be available for a limited time (banned practice 7); claiming to be about to cease trading or move premises (banned practice 17); creating the false impression that the consumer has won a prize (banned practice 22); describing a product as free when the consumer has to pay more than unavoidable costs (banned practice 23); presenting rights given to consumers by law as a distinctive feature of the offer (banned practice 11).

Step 3 (Doc 11 and Doc 9): In ascending order the paragraphs are 5, 6, 7, 11, 17, 22, 23.

Decoy (Doc 9): banned practice 21, claiming to offer a competition or prize promotion without awarding the prizes, also concerns prizes, but the guide's clause is about telling someone they have won, which is banned practice 22.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Linking across documents
How a careless solver fails: Wrong members in a list
Where the trap sits: Doc 11, the short business guide, lists seven behaviours under its theme heading "Misleading
promotional claims". Its prize clause sits in the sentence "You mustn’t say a product is free when in fact it isn’t, or
tell someone they have won a prize if that’s not true." The guide prints no paragraph numbers, so each behaviour has to
be matched to one of the 32 numbered banned practices in chapter 3 of Doc 9. Only two of those practices mention a
prize, and they sit side by side. Banned practice 21 comes first, and its text holds the first "prize" in Doc 9:
"Claiming to offer a competition or prize promotion without awarding the prizes described or a reasonable equivalent."
Its example is a scratch-card promotion whose £10,000 top prize could never be awarded. Banned practice 22 follows:
"Creating the false impression that the consumer has already won, will win, or will on doing a particular act win, a
prize or other equivalent benefit".
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: 5, 6, 7, 11, 17, 21, 22, 23. The solver matches the other six behaviours
correctly: bait advertising to 5, bait and switch to 6, the special offer that will "finish soon" to 7, statutory rights
to 11, ceasing trading or moving premises to 17, and "free" to 23. For the prize clause it searches Doc 9 for "prize",
finds both prize practices and keeps both, treating a prize promotion that never pays out as one more false prize claim.
That gives eight paragraphs for the guide's seven behaviours.
Why the correct answer avoids it: A careful reader compares what each prize practice forbids. The guide's clause is a
false statement to a consumer that they have won. That is banned practice 22: a false impression of having won, when
in fact "there is no prize or other equivalent benefit" or claiming it "requires the consumer to pay money or incur a
cost". Its example is a letter stating that "the consumer has won a prize". Banned practice 21 forbids a different act:
claiming to offer a competition or prize promotion and then not awarding the prizes described. It does not involve
telling anyone they have won, and nothing in the guide's paragraph mentions competitions or prizes that go unawarded,
so no clause matches 21. Each of the
guide's seven behaviours matches exactly one practice, and Doc 9 numbers its practices as Schedule 20 does ("paragraph 13
of Schedule 20" for banned practice 13). The answer is therefore seven paragraphs: 5, 6, 7, 11, 17, 22, 23. The guide's
only other mention of prizes, "aids to help people win prizes when gambling", sits under a different theme, "Illegal
products".
