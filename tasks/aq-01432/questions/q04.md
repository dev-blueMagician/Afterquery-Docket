## Question

The UK's fake-review guidance illustrates prohibited services offered to traders. If each were actually provided, which, if any, would expose the provider to liability under the FTC's rule on consumer reviews and testimonials, as FTC staff interpret it? List them in any order.

## Answer

Social media groups recruiting people to post fake reviews, selling bot-generated reviews, an SEO service that generates fake reviews

## Components

- c1: Social media groups recruiting people to post fake reviews
- c2: selling bot-generated reviews
- c3: an SEO service that generates fake reviews

## Reasoning

Step 1 (Doc 5): The UK fake-review guidance gives two groups of prohibited services for traders. Under "Offering to procure banned reviews and information for traders" it lists running social media groups that recruit individuals willing to post fake or concealed incentivised reviews, selling reviews generated by software applications such as bots, and a search engine optimisation service whose means include generating fake reviews; it says this element targets "the business model of acting as a broker procuring banned reviews and information". Under "Offering services that facilitate" it gives services that help fake reviews get through: expertise in bypassing a platform's fake review detection, services that enable accounts to be set up so as to avoid detection, and running a platform that allows such services to be sold.

Step 2 (Doc 2): FTC staff say advertising agencies, public relations firms, review brokers and reputation management companies "could be liable under Section 465.2(a) if they write, create, or sell a fake or false consumer review", and that "Brokers of fake reviews would generally fall under Section 465.2(a)'s prohibition against selling a consumer review, given that such brokers are generally being paid to provide fake reviews."

Step 3 (Doc 5 and Doc 2): The three procurement services, once provided, write, create or sell fake reviews: the recruiting groups broker fake reviews for pay, the bot service sells fake reviews, and the search engine optimisation service generates them. Each would expose its provider to liability under Section 465.2(a). For the recruiting groups the fake-review branch is enough: recruiting people to post fake reviews brokers fake reviews, so the provider is exposed whether or not its other branch, concealed incentivised reviews, would be.

Step 4 (Doc 2): The facilitation services write, create, sell or buy no review. The other routes FTC staff name for such firms are incentives tied to sentiment (Section 465.4), review suppression (Section 465.7(a)) and misusing fake indicators of social media influence (Section 465.8). Staff say Section 465.8 "focuses on the 'distribution' of fake indicators of social media influence" to people who could use them to misrepresent their influence, such as fake followers. Setting up accounts that avoid detection, or helping fake reviews past detection, distributes no such indicators. The platform example only lets others sell such services for reviews posted on other sites; it writes, creates, sells or buys no review either, and staff say even a business that hosts reviews is not liable for them "assuming you didn't write or buy them". None of the facilitation services is covered.

Step 5 (Doc 5 and Doc 2): The services that would expose the provider to liability are the social media recruiting groups, the sale of bot-generated reviews and the search engine optimisation service that generates fake reviews.

Decoy (Doc 2): staff report that the rule defines fake indicators of social media influence as ones "generated by bots, purported individual accounts not associated with a real individual", so a reader matching words puts the account set-up service under Section 465.8; but that provision concerns selling or distributing the indicators themselves, and the service only enables accounts to be set up.

## Targeted failure modes

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Linking across documents
How a careless solver fails: Misapplies a rule or premise
Where the trap sits: Doc 5, the UK fake-review guidance, gives as an example of a prohibited facilitation service
"Offering services to traders that enable accounts to be set up in a way that avoids or reduces the risk of detection."
Doc 2, FTC staff's answers on the US consumer reviews rule, lists the routes by which review brokers and similar firms
can be liable, including misusing "fake or false indicators of social media influence" under Section 465.8, and
reports that "the rule defines" those indicators as ones "generated by bots, purported individual accounts not
associated with a real individual, accounts created with a real individual’s personal information without their
consent, or hijacked accounts". The word "accounts" ties the UK example to that definition.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: Social media groups recruiting people to post fake reviews, selling
bot-generated reviews, an SEO service that generates fake reviews, and services that set up accounts to avoid
detection. The solver keeps the three procurement services under Section 465.2(a), then checks the facilitation
examples against the other routes staff list. It finds "accounts" in the fake-indicator definition, reads Section
465.8 as reaching anyone who helps create such accounts, and adds the account set-up service as a fourth item.
Why the correct answer avoids it: A careful reader checks what Section 465.8 makes unlawful. FTC staff say the
provision "focuses on the “distribution” of fake indicators of social media influence", that is, distributing them to
people "who could use the indicators to misrepresent their influence", and their examples are fake followers. The
indicators are the followers and similar signals that such accounts generate. The UK example offers to "enable
accounts to be set up", in the guidance's chapter on helping banned reviews get published, and distributes no
indicator. It also writes, creates and sells no review, so Section 465.2(a) does not reach it either. Only the three
procurement services, an element the UK guidance says targets "the business model of acting as a broker procuring
banned reviews", write, create or sell fake reviews.
