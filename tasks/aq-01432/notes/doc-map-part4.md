### D09 Overview (L13-L115) - key statements

- L15 "The Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act) contains provisions to
  protect consumers from unfair trading.[footnote 1] These provisions replace and update the
  existing Consumer Protection from Unfair Trading Regulations 2008 (CPUTRs),[footnote 2] with
  some changes.[footnote 3]"
- L17 "These provisions of the DMCC Act apply to commercial practices which happen from 6 April
  2025 onwards. They maintain much of the scope and effect of the previous law. Traders can
  generally expect the same or similar requirements and prohibitions to apply ... as under the
  previous regulations and consumers can expect a similar level of protection. The DMCC Act
  also includes some new or more detailed prohibitions of practices relating to fake consumer
  reviews[footnote 4] and 'drip pricing'.[footnote 5]" (hedge "generally" attaches to the
  continuity expectation).
- L19 purpose: help traders comply; "also be of use to legal advisers, enforcers and consumer
  advisers" [fn 6, 7].
- L21 replaces OFT1008 (see edition relationship above).
- L25 "The UCP provisions apply to the commercial dealings that businesses, referred to in the
  UCP provisions as 'traders', have with consumers. Such dealings are called 'commercial
  practices'. They include anything traders do that could in some way affect consumers and the
  decisions they take." Core concepts named: 'trader', 'commercial practices', 'consumer',
  'average consumer', 'transactional decisions'.
- L27 short trader definition: "A person is a 'trader' if they conduct activities relating to
  their business or on behalf of another person's business." Four example activities L31-L43
  (supply own products / promote or supply another trader's products / buy products from
  consumers / help consumers sell to others, including other consumers).
- L45 UCP provisions prohibit practices "which involve a trader misleading consumers, behaving
  aggressively, or otherwise acting unfairly towards consumers."
- Prohibition-to-section map (L47-L79):
  - L49 "Schedule 20 of the DMCC Act covers 32 specified practices which are in all
    circumstances considered unfair. Go to chapter 3".
  - L51 "Section 230 of the Act covers the prohibited conduct of omitting any information that
    must be contained in an invitation to purchase[footnote 8]. Go to chapter 4".
  - L53-L63 Section 226 (4 items: false or misleading information / deceptive overall

### D09 chapter 2, core concepts (L117-L383) - definitions (verbatim) and scope

- L121-L131 concept list (5 bullets): trader / commercial practice / consumer / transactional
  decision / "average consumer, vulnerable consumer and targeted consumer". L135 "The concepts
  discussed in this chapter are defined in the DMCC Act." [fn 12]
- TRADER L139-L147: "The UCP provisions define a trader as either: - a person ('P') acting for
  purposes relating to P's business - a person acting in the name of, or on behalf of, P for
  purposes relating to P's business [footnote 13]".
- BUSINESS L149: "'Business' in turn includes a trade, craft or profession (whether or not they
  are carried on for gain or reward), the activities of public bodies and the activities of
  others carried on for gain or reward. [footnote 14]"
- GAIN OR REWARD L151: "a broader concept than simply money paid for a product. It includes
  things like offering free gifts to consumers (for example to obtain goodwill or reputational
  advantage) or providing services in exchange for personal data."
- Trader capture list L153-L169 (4 items): agents/subcontractors/representatives/associates
  [fn 15] (L157); individuals regularly selling from home through online marketplaces (L161:
  "Someone who purchases items in order to resell them would be likely to fall within the
  definition. An individual person selling unwanted goods on the internet from time to time,
  would not be likely to fall within this definition"); charitable/ethical organisations
  (charity shop, asking for donations, L165); public bodies (passport fee, driving licence
  renewal, L169).
- L171 case-by-case; factors "likely to be relevant": for profit; number and frequency of
  transactions; time between purchase and sale. "A person may be a trader in some transactions
  and a consumer in other transactions."
- COMMERCIAL PRACTICE L175-L181: "A commercial practice is an act or omission by a trader
  relating to the promotion or supply of either: - the trader's product to a consumer - another
  trader's product to a consumer - a consumer's product to the trader or another person
  [footnote 16]".
- PRODUCT L183-L211: "covers both physical and intangible things, including goods (including
  immovable property, rights and obligations), services and digital content." Seven listed
  kinds: physical items (pencil, car) L187; digital content (music purchased online) L191;
  services ("a right to use a caravan for a certain period") L195; immovable property (sale or
  lease of land) L199; intangible rights (cancellation or cashback options) L203; credit rights
  (debt collection and payment demands) L207; membership rights (club) L211.
- L213 "A commercial practice is each single act or omission, whether or not these are repeated
  or form part of a course of conduct. It does not matter whether the acts or omissions happen
  before, during or after the promotion or supply in question.[footnote 17]" Includes
  advertising, sale and supply, post-contractual matters (after-sales, debt collection).
- L215 "relate to"; rule of thumb - even if not selling directly to consumers. L217 online
  platforms facilitating traders "are within the scope". L219 consumer-to-trader (used car,
  antique, donated clothes) and consumer-to-consumer via online marketplace are covered. L221
  several traders in a chain each covered; choice of party to investigate.
- SCOPE - NOT COVERED (L225-L231), heading "Business-to-business practices with no potential to
  affect consumers": (a) specialist tractor parts sold to businesses only - "does not need to
  consider compliance with the UCP provisions. The trader should however consider the Business
  Protection from Misleading Marketing Regulations 2008.[footnote 18]" (L229); (b) mattresses to
  hotels with no consumer claims - same, and "The hotels must comply with the UCP provisions in
  their dealings with consumers." (L231).
- SCOPE - COVERED (L233-L247): spare computer parts online "if consumers may buy" (L237);
  waterproof clothing supplier to 'eco-friendly' retailers, claim of "a specified minimum
  percentage of recycled material" - claims to retailers must be truthful (L239); trader's
  statements about the value of a car it might buy from a consumer (L243); online marketplace
  featured deals - "they may be carrying out a banned practice if they falsely state that a
  particular featured appliance is available for a limited time only" (L247).
- CONSUMER L251: "The UCP provisions define a consumer as an individual acting for purposes that
  are wholly or mainly outside the individual's business.[footnote 19]" L253 dual purposes - "as
  long as the consumer purpose is the main purpose.[footnote 20]" Examples: kettle bought for
  home, works from home "one day a week" - "would still be a consumer" [fn 21] (L257);
  occasional seller of used clothes / personal vinyl record collection "is also likely to be a
  consumer" [fn 22] (L261); investor in shares or property "could still be a consumer" (L265).
  L267 sole trader/partner protected only "where, and to the extent that" acting outside the
  business; printer "of which 95% of the intended use is for the purposes of their business, is
  unlikely to be a consumer for the purposes of this purchase.[footnote 23]"
- TRANSACTIONAL DECISION L271-L285: "any decision made by a consumer relating to: - the purchase
  or supply of a product (including whether, how or on what terms to make the purchase or
  supply) - the retention, disposal or withdrawal of a product (including whether, how or on
  what terms to retain or dispose of it) - the exercise of contractual rights in relation to a
  product (including whether, how or on what terms to exercise such rights)[footnote 25]".
  L287 no actual transaction needed; includes not doing something [fn 26]; before, during and
  after a contract [fn 27]. L289-L305 eight non-exhaustive examples (visit a shop / click
  through / agree to a sales presentation or view a property / buy / sell / enter a contract /
  exercise cancellation, refund, replacement or after-sales right / "whether to pay a debt"
  [fn 28]).
- AVERAGE CONSUMER - three reference types L309-L315: the test for "professional diligence or
  the prohibitions of misleading and aggressive practices" is judged "(as appropriate)" by the
  'average consumer' / 'average member' of a targeted group / 'average member' of a vulnerable
  group [fn 29]. L317-L319 diagram (alt text only) of the "3 types of consumers".
  - L321 targeted group -> "This type of average consumer is referred to in this Guidance as the
    'average targeted consumer'."
  - L323 vulnerable group "in a way that the trader could reasonably be expected to foresee".
  - L325 "The average consumer is a notional construct. There is no requirement to show evidence
    of actual consumers being affected by an unfair commercial practice." Decision-makers: "the
    court and the CMA (as the case may be)".
- VULNERABLE CONSUMER L329-L337 (three conditions): "there is a group of consumers / the group
  is particularly vulnerable to a commercial practice / the trader could reasonably be expected
  to foresee that the group of consumers is vulnerable to the commercial practice, then the
  relevant average consumer is the average member of that group.[footnote 30]" L341 vulnerable
  to the practice or "because of the underlying product" [fn 31]. L343 "The test is objective.
  It is not necessary that the trader actually foresees the effect". L345 vulnerable "if (among
  other things) they are vulnerable because of age, physical or mental health, credulity or the
  circumstances they are in.[footnote 32]" Four sub-headings: Age L347-L349 (elderly / burglar
  alarm sales; children / toys on daytime television or apps); Mental or physical health
  L351-L353 (wheelchair users and access claims; hearing difficulties and 'hearing aid
  compatibility'; consumers who cannot see; eating disorders and weight-management claims);
  Credulity L355-L357 ("The term is neutral"; environmental claims [fn 33]); Circumstances
  L359-L361 (mourning, divorce, losing a job; training adverts for the recently redundant).
  L363 "These categories are non-exhaustive and are not mutually exclusive" (neurodiversity
  example). L365 evidenced processes; "traders must also consider the requirements of the
  Equality Act 2010."
- AVERAGE TARGETED CONSUMER L369: "assumed to be reasonably well informed and reasonably
  observant and circumspect, subject to the characteristics of that group.[footnote 34]" L371
  indications (placement, language, nature of product, context). Two examples L375-L377
  (children's programmes / online games; credit for 'non-status' or 'sub-prime' borrowers).
- GENERAL AVERAGE CONSUMER L381: "The average consumer is defined as 'reasonably well informed,
  reasonably observant and reasonably circumspect'.[footnote 35] The average consumer cannot be
  expected to do their own research to find out any relevant information if the trader hides
  it.[footnote 36]" L383 "likely to not be a technical expert, nor does 'average' mean a
  statistically average consumer. The definition is not a statistical test or statistical
  average.[footnote 37]"

  12. L517-L519 paid-for editorial content not made clear. Ex L523: online influencer "gifted a

### D09 chapter 4, omission of material information from an invitation to purchase (L765-L927)

- Summary L769: "When a trader gives information to consumers about a product and its price,
  this will normally be an 'invitation to purchase'. An invitation to purchase does not need to
  include an actual opportunity to enable the consumer to purchase the product (eg a TV, radio,
  print or online advertisement)." (hedge "normally" attaches to the classification.)
- L771 material-information categories "(unless already apparent from the context)": main
  characteristics and total price; trader identity and business address; cancellation rights;
  information required under other legislation.
- L773 (effect test NOT required): "Failure to give the required material information clearly,
  in a timely manner, or in a way that the consumer is likely to see it, is unlawful regardless
  of whether that is likely to affect consumers' decisions."
- L775 drip pricing: "The practice of showing consumers an initial headline price for a product
  and subsequently introducing additional mandatory charges as consumers proceed with a purchase
  or transaction - sometimes called 'drip pricing' - is prohibited."
- L779 "The omission of material information from an invitation to purchase is an unfair
  practice regardless of whether it is likely to impact a consumer's transactional decision.
  [footnote 46] This contrasts with the misleading omissions prohibition described in chapter 6
  (Misleading omissions)."
- INVITATION TO PURCHASE (L783-L813): L785 "When a trader gives information to consumers about a
  product and its price, this will normally be an 'invitation to purchase'.[footnote 47]" Eight
  listed examples L789-L803: price on a product in a shop; website/marketplace listing or item in
  an online 'basket'; restaurant menu (physical card or QR-code digital menu); TV, radio or
  social media advert; email or text promotion; newspaper car advert with a "'drive away from'
  price"; online advertisement; in-app banner for an ad-free version or in-app purchases. L805
  can exist before an in-principle decision [fn 48] and before purchase is possible; L807 no
  purchase opportunity needed [fn 49] (train-carriage poster example); L809 minimal or
  incomplete information suffices, e.g. lowest price or single visual reference [fn 50]; L811
  multiple points; L813 directed at UK consumers "even if the trader making the invitation to
  purchase is located outside the UK" [fn 51].
- NOT an invitation to purchase (L815-L819): L817 "A commercial practice which does not include
  a price is not an invitation to purchase. Where a commercial practice does not indicate the
  characteristics of a specific product, through text, image or otherwise, it will not be an
  invitation to purchase. In many cases, advertisements which promote a trader's 'brand' rather
  than any particular product(s) will not be invitations to purchase." L819 such a practice may
  still breach the misleading omission rule "if it is likely consumers would take a different
  transactional decision" [fn 52].
- Responsibility (L821-L845): L823 the trader making the invitation is responsible "even if they
  are not the person actually selling the product". Four examples L827-L833 (brand owner sold by
  third-party retailers/franchisees; price comparison websites; online marketplace operator;
  influencer). L835 seller "may also be responsible"; L837 marketplace operator and seller "may
  be held responsible"; L841-L843 two duties for traders using other businesses to market;
  L845 exclusion: "A trader is not liable for the actions of an independent third party, simply
  because that third party has purchased the trader's product and is reselling it."
- Required information - section 230(2) list "summarised below" (L849-L901). Top-level bullets
  (10): L853 main characteristics [fn 53] ("should correspond to the price indicated" - basic
  model example); L857 "either the total price of the product (including any mandatory fees,
  taxes, charges or other payments that the consumer must pay if they purchase the product);
  [footnote 54] or" ; L861 where the total price cannot reasonably be calculated in advance, the
  way it will be calculated [fn 55]; L865 optional freight, delivery or postal charges not in the
  total price (or the fact that they may be payable) [fn 56]; L869 identity of the trader and of
  any principal [fn 57]; L873 trader contact information - three sub-items: place-of-business
  address (L877), address for service of documents if different (L881), "any existing email
  address used by the trader for conducting business" [fn 58] (L885); L889 principal's contact
  information [fn 59]; L893 existence of withdrawal or cancellation rights [fn 60]; L897
  different arrangements for payment, delivery, performance [fn 61] or complaint handling
  [fn 62]; L901 information required by any other legislative provision [fn 63] (last item).
- L903 "The invitation to purchase must provide all the material information [footnote 64]
  clearly, in a timely way and in a way that the consumer is likely to see it. A trader that
  fails to do so will be considered to have omitted that information, even though it has been
  provided in some form. [footnote 65]"
- L905 cross-reference: "the CMA's Unfair commercial practices: price transparency guidance
  (CMA209)" (drip pricing and partitioned pricing).
- Means-of-communication limits (L907-L927): "2 factors" (L909) - limitations of space/time
  [fn 66] (L915) and steps to overcome them [fn 67] (L919). L921 hedge and rule: "limitations of
  time and space will generally be unlikely to justify omitting required pricing information
  from an invitation to purchase ... a single price figure inclusive of all mandatory charges
  will be able to fit on or within all types of devices and media." L923 required information
  "should not be omitted in favour of other, non-mandatory, information." L925 "by exception"
  other means (hyperlink, QR code, website) [fn 68, 69]. L927 apparent from context (shop
  address when in the shop; branded streaming-service advert identifies the trader).

### D09 chapter 5, misleading actions (L929-L1113)

- L933 "4 types of misleading action are prohibited if the average consumer is likely to take a

  misleadingly presented information [fn 71, 72]. L957 hedge: "Generally, the more important a

  address might be unlikely to cause the average consumer to take a different decision, even if
  it means the information was false." L959 "The UCP provisions do not expressly set out the
  types of information that are relevant ... Generally, however, the types of information which
  may impact ... include:[footnote 73]" - 21 bullet markers L963-L1043 (L963 "the main
  characteristics of the product, including:" then L967-L1043; first sub-items availability/
  quantity/specification, benefits or risks [fn 74], fitness for purpose, usage/results,
  execution [fn 75]/composition/accessories, origin, method and date of manufacture, tests,

  motives [fn 77], nature of the sales process, sponsorship/approval statements or symbols,
  price or manner of calculation, specific price advantage, need for a service/part/replacement/
  repair, nature/attributes/rights of the trader, consumer's rights or risks (last, L1043)).
  NESTING DEPTH not recoverable from the flat text (where the "main characteristics" sub-list
  ends is not marked). L1045 consumer's rights may include "Parts 1 and 2, Chapter 5 of Part 3
  and Schedules 2 and 3 to the Consumer Rights Act 2015" and "The Package Travel and Linked
  Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018". Examples (2): L1049 'premium' streaming package falsely
  said to include key channels; L1051 house advertised "as having 3 bedrooms, when in fact it has
