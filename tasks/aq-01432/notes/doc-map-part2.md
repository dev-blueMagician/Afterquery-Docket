### D05 introduction (L15-L27)

- L15-L17 premise; "endorsements must be honest and not misleading. An endorsement must reflect
  the honest opinion of the endorser and can't be used to make a claim the marketer of the
  product couldn't legally make." (L17)
- L19 disclosure principle: "if there's a connection between an endorser and the marketer that a
  significant minority of consumers wouldn't expect and it would affect how they evaluate the
  endorsement, that connection should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously." Relative or
  employee -> "the ad is misleading unless the connection is made clear"; paid/given something of
  value -> "The same is usually true" (hedge "usually" attaches to the paid/given-value case).
- L21 resort example: YouTube video review; hotel paid the reviewer "hundreds of dollars" or the
  reviewer "had stayed there for several days for free".
- L23 atypical-results example: endorser "lost 20 pounds in two months"; regimen "by just taking a
  pill daily for two months"; without proof of typicality the ad "must make clear to the audience
  what the generally expected results of following that same regimen are."
- L25 audience: "advertisers, ad agencies, influencers, bloggers, and others"; scope disclaimer:
  "Our staff guidance can't be definitive"; "This guidance doesn't provide a safe harbor from
  potential liability; whether a particular advertising claim is deceptive or otherwise violates
  the FTC Act will depend on the facts of the specific case."
- L27 change statement (2023 revision of the Guides, quoted above) and two other FTC documents
  named: "Disclosures 101 for Social Media Influencers" and "Soliciting and Paying for Online
  Reviews: A Guide for Marketers".

### D05 question index, section 1: About the Endorsement Guides (L61-L105)

- L63 What is an endorsement? -> L65 DEFINITION: "As used in the Guides, an endorsement is an
  advertising message that consumers are likely to believe reflects the opinions or beliefs of
  someone other than the sponsoring advertiser. So, if the message isn't an advertising message by
  or on behalf of a marketer, it isn't an endorsement." (contrasted with the dictionary sense)
- L67 Do the Guides apply to social media? -> L69 Yes.
- L71 Isn't it common knowledge influencers are paid? -> L73-L75: some have no connection;
  "Under the law, an act or practice is deceptive if it misleads "a significant minority" of
  consumers." (L75)
- L77 Are you monitoring influencers and bloggers? -> L79: case by case; "our focus usually will
  be on advertisers or their ad agencies and public relations firms. However, action against an
  individual endorser might be appropriate in certain circumstances - for example, if the endorser
  hasn't made required disclosures despite warnings." (hedges "usually", "might")
- L81 Higher standard than traditional media reviewers? -> L83-L85 No; "The FTC Act applies across
  the board"; newspaper/TV-employed reviewer's audience "probably understands" the relationship.
- L87 Legal basis? -> L89-L91: "Section 5 of the FTC Act, which generally prohibits deceptive
  advertising"; "The Guides themselves don't have the force of law. However, practices
  inconsistent with the Guides may result in law enforcement actions alleging Section 5
  violations."; orders can require paying money back to consumers; "if the defendants received a
  Notice of Penalty Offenses regarding endorsements and then engaged in certain deceptive or unfair
  endorsement practices, the FTC could seek substantial civil penalties." (L91)
- L93 "viewers" vs "followers"? -> L95: non-followers' interpretations matter if they can see
  posts; followers more likely to see and trust.
- L97 Political advertisements? -> L99 SCOPE EXCLUSION: "The FTC doesn't have jurisdiction over
  political advertisements."
- L101 Brand hires several influencers to post brand-created content (e.g. "a picture for a
  Facebook post or pre-written text for a tweet")? -> L103-L105: no blanket prohibition; misleading
  if picture conveys use/experience that isn't true; pre-written posts OK "as long as the
  influencers are being truthful."

### D05 question index, section 2: When Does the FTC Act Apply to Product Recommendations? (L107-L229)

- L109 Blogger: must I always say whether free or paid? -> L111-L113 No. Not covered: product you
  paid for; free samples a store gives its customers ("your recommendation isn't a commercial
  practice covered by the FTC Act"). Covered: advertiser (or someone working for one) pays/gives
  something of value; free products or perks "with the expectation that you'll promote or discuss";
  "Bloggers who are part of network marketing programs, where they sign up to receive free product
  samples in exchange for writing about them".
- L115 YouTube video endorsing several products? -> L117 Yes; same guidance whatever the format.
- L119 "$1-off coupon, an entry in a sweepstakes or a contest, or a product worth only a few
  dollars"? -> L121-L125: depends on effect on weight/credibility; sweepstakes "to win a thousand
  dollars" could affect; "It's always safer to disclose"; "continually getting free stuff" could
  suggest expected future benefits; "Even an incentive with no financial value might affect the
  weight or credibility" - Guides' example of a restaurant patron offered the chance to appear in
  a TV ad. Extraction note: L121 opens a parenthesis "(See the Q&A below in the section entitled
  "Social Media Contests."" that is never closed (source text as captured).
- L127 Company donates to charity whenever someone reviews? -> L129 "we suggest erring on the side
  of caution and disclosing".
- L131 Lent product returned after review? -> L133 "That might depend on the product and how long

  though you have to return it".
- L135 Signed a one-year contract two months ago; followers "might have seen eight disclosures";

  deceptive because viewers might not have seen the prior posts."
- L139 Free "$60 video game (and nothing else)" a year ago for live streaming? -> L141 depends on

- L143 Influencer later buys the once-free product with own money? -> L145-L147: ongoing

- L149 Local-restaurant review site with display ads; free meals? -> L151 disclose free meals;

- L153 New restaurant inviting family and friends to eat free? -> L155 "two issues": disclose

- L157 Hunting/camping YouTube channel; knife manufacturers send free knives; no obligation? ->
  L159 "So we advise disclosing that fact."
- L161 Paid video review that also covers negatives? -> L163 "Even if your review mentions one or
  more negative points, it's still an endorsement."; disclose payment.
- L165 Skin care influencer with a brand deal criticises a competing brand? -> L167 Yes, disclose.
- L169 Paid for Facebook and Instagram; free extra endorsement on Snapchat? -> L171 Yes.
- L173 Trade association "ambassador" promoting its conference "primarily on Twitter", paid "for
  five hours a week"; off-time replies; badge in Twitter profile? -> L175-L179: relationship exists

  "merely giving someone a requested link to the conference agenda"); email/text reply "probably"
  no disclosure; profile badge "wouldn't be sufficient".
- L181 Blogger flown to a launch and put up "in a hotel for a couple of nights", nothing else? ->
  L183 Yes, disclose "free travel and accommodations".
- L185 Posts mention/show products but say nothing positive? -> L187 a picture (e.g. Pinterest) or
  video "could convey - even without words" approval -> endorsement; no relationship -> not subject

- L189 Instagram photo tagging the dress brand, nothing said? -> L191 "Tagging a brand you're

- L193 Untagged dress, picture at a restaurant? -> L195 "Probably not."; cites an analogous

- L197 Car company pays blogger to write about wanting an electric sports car? -> L199 Yes; "An

- L205 Authors' group swapping book reviews, no money? -> L207 Yes, disclose; "the need to make a

- L209 Facebook page names my employer; extra disclosure? -> L211 Yes.
- L213 Famous athlete, "millions of followers on Twitter", known spokesperson? -> L215-L217 depends;

- L223 Video blogger living in London, sponsored YouTube videos for products also sold in the

### D05 question index, section 3: Product Placements (L231-L245)

- L233 Product placements on TV shows? -> L235 "Federal Communications Commission law (FCC, not
  FTC) requires TV stations to include disclosures of product placement in TV shows."; L237 FTC
  opinion: product placement ("merely showing products or brands in third-party entertainment
  content, as distinguished from sponsored content or disguised commercials") "doesn't require a
  disclosure that the advertiser paid for the placement."
- L239 Two TV talk show hosts paid to promote a video game, humorous segment, not experts? -> L241
  endorsements, "more than a mere product placement"; humour/expertise irrelevant; exception: a host
  saying something "obviously an advertisement" -> disclosure "probably isn't necessary" (example
  "Wake up with EFG Coffee. It's how I start my day!").
- L243 Talk show "bargains segment" partnered with brand(s)? -> L245 not just placement; disclose;
  "To be most effective, the disclosures should be presented at the beginning of the segment and
  when specific products are endorsed"; avoid "we've partnered with vendors."

### D05 question index, section 4: Endorsements by Ordinary Consumers on Social Media (L247-L263)

- L249 Clicking a button/sharing a link as a fan? -> L251 own purchase, no relationship -> none;
  sponsored campaign or compensation (e.g. "getting a discount on a future purchase or being entered
  into a sweepstakes for a significant prize") -> "a disclosure is appropriate".
- L253 Rewards for online brand campaigns: "like", pin, share? -> L255 pin/share in a sponsored
  campaign "probably requires a disclosure"; L257 "Facebook's "like" buttons" cannot carry a

### D05 question index, section 5: How Should I Disclose ... (L265-...)

- L267 Is tagging a brand a sufficient disclosure? -> L269 "No. Tagging a brand is an endorsement,
  but it's not a disclosure that you have a connection to a brand."
- L271 Special wording required? -> L273 No; examples "This is an ad for BRAND", "This video is paid

- L275 Need a lawyer? -> L277 No; examples "Company X gave me [name of product] to try, and I think

- L279 Do the example disclosures work for children and teens? -> L281 No; "children and teens can

- L283 List details of everything received? -> L285 No; "whether you got $100 or $1,000, you could

- L287 How small is too small to matter? -> L289 "there is no threshold amount that would apply in
  all situations"; may vary by endorser and product type.
- L291 Always disclose a free product; say more? -> L293-L297: "Saying only that you got a product

- L299 Unreleased video game, paid review; "sneak peek" enough? -> L301 No; "You should say that you

- L303 Single home-page disclosure "many products discussed on this site are provided free by their

- L307 TikTok disclosure in the video's text description? -> L309 "A disclosure in the text

- L315 YouTube: disclosure in the video description? -> L317 "You can, but that's not enough on
  its own"; best "if it's included in the video itself".
- L319 Instagram post description? -> L321-L323 streams show "only the first two or three lines";
  disclosure should appear without clicking "more"; picture-only endorsement may need a disclosure

- L325 Web-page button "DISCLOSURE" or "LEGAL" linking to full disclosure? -> L327 No; "easily

- L329 Tourism organization sponsoring an influencer trip; #ad on every post? -> L331 quotes ".com

### D05 section 8: Online Review Programs (L481-L513)
