Where the trap sits:

```text
Doc 11, the short business guide, lists seven behaviours under its theme heading "Misleading promotional claims". Its prize clause sits in the sentence "You mustn’t say a product is free when in fact it isn’t, or tell someone they have won a prize if that’s not true." The guide prints no paragraph numbers, so each behaviour has to be matched to one of the 32 numbered banned practices in chapter 3 of Doc 9. Only two of those practices mention a prize, and they sit side by side. Banned practice 21 comes first, and its text holds the first "prize" in Doc 9: "Claiming to offer a competition or prize promotion without awarding the prizes described or a reasonable equivalent." Its example is a scratch-card promotion whose £10,000 top prize could never be awarded. Banned practice 22 follows: "Creating the false impression that the consumer has already won, will win, or will on doing a particular act win, a prize or other equivalent benefit".
```

The wrong answer a careless solver gives:

```text

The solver matches the other six behaviours correctly: bait advertising to 5, bait and switch to 6, the special offer that will "finish soon" to 7, statutory rights to 11, ceasing trading or moving premises to 17, and "free" to 23. For the prize clause it searches Doc 9 for "prize", finds both prize practices and keeps both, treating a prize promotion that never pays out as one more false prize claim. That gives eight paragraphs for the guide's seven behaviours.
```

Why the correct answer avoids it:

```text
A careful reader compares what each prize practice forbids. The guide's clause is a false statement to a consumer that they have won. That is banned practice 22: a false impression of having won, when in fact "there is no prize or other equivalent benefit" or claiming it "requires the consumer to pay money or incur a cost". Its example is a letter stating that "the consumer has won a prize". Banned practice 21 forbids a different act: claiming to offer a competition or prize promotion and then not awarding the prizes described. It does not involve telling anyone they have won, and nothing in the guide's paragraph mentions competitions or prizes that go unawarded, so no clause matches 21. Each of the guide's seven behaviours matches exactly one practice, and Doc 9 numbers its practices as Schedule 20 does ("paragraph 13 of Schedule 20" for banned practice 13). The answer is therefore seven paragraphs: 5, 6, 7, 11, 17, 22, 23. The guide's only other mention of prizes, "aids to help people win prizes when gambling", sits under a different theme, "Illegal products".
```
