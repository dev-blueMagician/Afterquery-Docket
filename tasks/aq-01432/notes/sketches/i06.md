## Question

Under the UK's Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, UK guidance lists six legal provisions that a brand engaged in hidden advertising on social media may breach. For how many of those six could the brand be prosecuted?

## Answer

4

## Evidence

- D11 (para 17, L95-L98): "In particular, brands engaged in hidden advertising may breach the following provisions:"
- D11 (para 17, L98): "(i) Using editorial content in the media to promote a product without making it clear you have been paid (Banned Practice 12), (ii) commissioning or writing a consumer review that is fake or hides the fact it was incentivised  (Banned Practice 13) (ii) Creating the impression of being a consumer (Banned Practice 25), (iii) Professional diligence (sections 225 & 229), (iv) Misleading actions (sections 225 & 226) and (v) Misleading omissions (sections 225 & 227)."
- D09 (Offences, L1387-L1411): "It is an offence to engage in a commercial practice which involves:"
- D09 (fn112, L1885): "It is not a criminal offence to infringe paragraphs 12, 13 or 30 of Schedule 20."
- D09 (fn39, L1593): "Most banned practices (except those listed in paragraphs 12, 13 and 30 of Schedule 20 to the DMCC Act) amount to criminal offences"
- D09 (fn113, L1889): "It is not a criminal offence to fail to comply with a requirement in a code of conduct, contrary to section 226(1)(d) of the DMCC Act."
- D09 (fn114, L1893): "To be convicted of the offence of infringing professional diligence, it must be proven that the trader either knew or was reckless"
