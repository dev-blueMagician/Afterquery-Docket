## Question

UK fake-review guidance gives examples of traders commissioning reviews by offering customers or other consumers something. As FTC staff read the US consumer reviews rule, which of those examples would breach its ban on incentives tied to a review's sentiment? List them in any order.

## Answer

Free or discounted product for a five-star review, social media post offering free products for five-star reviews, reimbursing purchases once a positive review is submitted

## Evidence

- D04 (Commissioning examples, L287): "Offering an existing customer a free or discounted product in exchange for a five-star review (which is not reflective of their genuine experience) of their previous purchase."
- D04 (L291): "Sharing a post on social media asking for five-star reviews in exchange for free"
- D04 (L297-L299): "be reimbursed once they have submitted a positive review."
- D04 (L295): "Contacting a customer who has left a negative review and offering them a refund and/or a gift card if they change their review to remove the negative commentary"
- D03 (L143): "The rule does not prohibit giving incentives for reviews, as long as there isn’t an express or implied requirement that the reviews have to express a particular sentiment."
- D03 (L147): "No. That conduct would violate Section 465.4."
- D03 (L161-L163): "No, but paying consumers to change or remove truthful negative reviews may violate the FTC Act"
