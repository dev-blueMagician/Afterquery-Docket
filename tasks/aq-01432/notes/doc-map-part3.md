### Operative definitions (verbatim; line)

- "online" (n1, L142): "the term “online” includes advertising and marketing via the Internet and
  other electronic networks. It is device neutral and encompasses advertising and marketing on
  mobile devices, such as smartphones and tablets."
- Rules and Guides (L158-L160):
  - "Rules prohibit specific acts or practices that the Commission has found to be unfair or
    deceptive.4"
  - "Guides help businesses in their efforts to comply with the law by providing examples or
    direction on how to avoid unfair or deceptive acts or practices.5"
  - n5, L166: Guides are “administrative interpretations of laws administered by the Commission.”
- "substantiation" (L198): "Advertisers must have evidence to back up their claims
  (“substantiation”)".
- Deceptive (n11, L207): "an ad is deceptive if it contains a statement — or omits information —
  that is likely to mislead consumers acting reasonably under the circumstances and is “material” or
  important to a consumer’s decision to buy or use the product."
- Unfair (n13, L212): "an advertisement or business practice is unfair if it causes or is likely to
  cause substantial consumer injury that consumers could not reasonably avoid and that is not
  outweighed by the benefit to consumers or competition."
- Synonyms (n18, L255-L257): “clearly and prominently” = “clearly and conspicuously” "As used in FTC
  rules, guides, and cases".
- How the clear-and-conspicuous standard is measured (L244): "Whether a disclosure meets this
  standard is measured by its performance — that is, how consumers actually perceive and understand
  the disclosure within the context of the entire ad."
- Net impression (L245): "The key is the overall net impression of the ad — that is, whether the
  claims consumers take from the ad are truthful and substantiated."
- "triggering claim" (L317-L319): "the claim that raises the need for disclosure (often referred to
  as a “triggering claim”)".
- Hyperlink label (L388): "The hyperlink’s label — the text or graphic assigned to it —".
- Click-through page (L439): "The click-through page or screen — that is, the page or screen the
  hyperlink leads to — must contain the complete disclosure".
- Click-through rates (L444): "i.e., how often consumers click on a hyperlink and view the
  click-through information."
- Interstitials (L458): "interstitial pages that appear while another webpage is loading".
- Basic cost (L366): "a product’s basic cost (e.g., the cost of the item before taxes, shipping and
  handling, and any other fees are added on)".
- Space-constrained ads (L483): "Many space-constrained ads displayed today are teasers."
  Space-constrained ads include "banner ads and tweets".
- Augmented reality (L593): "augmented reality experiences (interactive computer-generated
  experiences)".
- "optimized" (n29, L557): a site version "designed or “optimized” to enable those consumers to view
  the site more easily."
- Responsive design (A-22, L832): "responsive design, which automatically detects the kind of device
  the consumer is using to access the site and arranges the content on the site so it makes sense
  for that device."
- Third-party online retailer (A-17, L787): "(a retailer that is not affiliated with the
  advertiser)".
- Negative option (A-23, L842): "the negative option in this example (enrollment in a recipe club)".
- Endorsement Guides scope (L190): "any advertising message . . . that consumers are likely to
  believe reflects the opinions, beliefs, findings, or experience of a party other than the
  sponsoring advertiser . . . ."
- Terms used for the share of consumers:
  - "a significant proportion of reasonable consumers" (L295)
  - "a significant minority of reasonable consumers" (L499, n27)
  - "a significant minority of consumers" (L620, n30)
  - "a significant proportion of reasonable viewers" (A-20, L810)

### Operative rules and scope exceptions (verbatim; line)

- Scope and status:
  - L138: "It does not, however, purport to cover every issue associated with online advertising
    disclosures, nor is it intended to provide a safe harbor from potential liability."
  - L138: "the ultimate test is whether the information intended to be disclosed is actually
    conveyed to consumers."
  - n2 (only FTC-enforced laws) and n3 (excluded industries).
  - n7, L180: "A rule or guide applies to online activities if its scope is not limited by how claims
    are communicated to consumers, how advertising is disseminated, or where commercial activities
    occur."
  - L156: "The Act is not limited to any particular medium."
  - L36: "including activities in the mobile marketplace."
  - n7, L183: rules using “written,” “writing,” and “printed” "For the most part" apply online.
    Email may be used "as long as consumers understand or expect to receive such information by
    email."
  - n7, L184: the Telemarketing Sales Rule covers "email and text messages that invite consumers to
    telephone the sender", "unless they qualify for the direct mail exemption under 16 C.F.R.
    310.6(b)(6) by clearly and conspicuously making certain specified disclosures in the original
    solicitation."
- A disclosure cannot cure (L223-L225): "A disclosure can only qualify or limit a claim to avoid a
  misleading impression. It cannot cure a false claim." When a disclosure contradicts a material
  claim, "the claim itself must be modified."
- Required disclosures (L227): "if a disclosure is required, it must be clear and conspicuous."
- If no clear disclosure is possible:
  - L253: "then either the claim should be modified so the disclosure is not necessary or the ad
    should not be disseminated."
  - L253/L269 (platforms): "if a particular platform does not provide an opportunity to make clear
    and conspicuous disclosures, it should not be used to disseminate advertisements that require
    such disclosures.22"
  - L493 (space-constrained ads): "the ad should be modified so it does not require such a
    disclosure or, if that is not possible, that space-constrained ad should not be used."
- Availability is not enough (L251): "Simply making the disclosure available somewhere in the ad,
  where some consumers might find it, does not meet the clear and conspicuous standard."
- Improve on evidence (L295-L297): "If there are indications that a significant proportion of
  reasonable consumers are not noticing or comprehending a necessary disclosure, the disclosure
  should be improved."
- Scrolling:
  - Text cues must be specific (L334). "see below for important information on restocking fees" is
    adequate, while “details below” is "not adequate cues".
  - "Scroll bars along the edges of a screen are not a sufficiently effective visual cue." (L340)
  - Ideally unavoidable (L347).
  - Mobile optimisation "will eliminate the need for consumers to scroll right or left, although it
    will not necessarily address the need for vertical scrolling." (L355)
- Hyperlinks:
  - Rule (L364): "Disclosures that are an integral part of a claim or inseparable from it should not
    be communicated through a hyperlink." It applies "particularly" to cost and health and safety
    disclosures (L366).
  - Exception (L368): "if the details about the additional fees are too complex to describe adjacent
    to the price claim, those details may be provided by using a hyperlink." The example label is
    “Service plan required. Get service plan prices.” (L374), and the link should be adjacent to the
    price.
  - L374: all cost information should be shown "clearly and conspicuously prior to purchase."
  - Exception to the importance of the label (L396): the label "may be less important" when seeing
    the disclosure is unavoidable, e.g., the product "can only be purchased online and the consumer
    must click on that link to proceed to a transaction".
  - Consistent styles (L415-L431). For example, if links are usually underlined, italicised text
    may not be recognised.
  - The click-through page "must contain the complete disclosure"; the link should go "directly to
    the disclosure" (L439-L441).
  - Monitoring tools are optional (L444). "If hyperlinks are not followed, another method of
    conveying the required information would be necessary." (L446-L448)
- Pop-ups:
  - Not blockable (L462-L464).
  - Unblockable ones "may be problematic"; the fix is an affirmative choice between "yes" and "no"
    buttons "without use of preselected buttons" (L466-L467).
  - Flash: "a disclosure that requires Adobe Flash Player will not be displayed on certain mobile
    devices." (L460)
- Before purchase (L471-L472): "Disclosures must be effectively communicated to consumers before
  they make a purchase or incur a financial obligation". The order screen alone may not be enough
  (L478).
- Other outlets (L480): when the product is available from "brick and mortar stores or from online
  retailers other than the advertiser itself, necessary disclosures should be made in the ad before
  consumers go to other outlets". See also L96 and L487-L493.
- Space-constrained ads:
  - "the advertiser disseminating it is not exempt from disclosure requirements" (L483)
  - put the disclosure in "each and every ad" (L497)
  - republication: put the disclosure at the beginning, or leave "enough free space" (L503)
  - exceptions allowing click-through disclosure (L513): "when a teaser ad does not actually identify
    the product being advertised ... or when the advertised product is sold only through the
    advertiser’s own website"; "The full disclosure must then be clearly and conspicuously displayed
    on the website."
  - interactive ads with in-ad purchase: "all required disclosures should be included in the ad
    itself" (L515)
- Prominence:
  - size "at least as large as the claim" (L524-L526)
  - contrast colour (L528-L530)
  - graphics not required (L533)
  - device rule with an exception (L548): "unless a website defaults to a mobile-optimized (or
    similarly responsive) version"
  - "terms of use": "should not be relegated to them" (L552); clicking “agree” "does not make the
    disclosure clear and conspicuous" (L552)
  - negative-option or other-subject disclosures need affirmative acknowledgement, "none of which is
    preselected", shown "early in the decision-making process, e.g., before the primary item is
    actually added to a shopping cart" (L555-L563)
- Repetition (L577-L590): "need not be repeated so often that consumers would ignore it or it would
  clutter the ad". Some disclosures "must always accompany the claim". Otherwise a clearly labelled
  hyperlink may be repeated so that "the full disclosure would be placed on only one page of the
  site."
- Multimedia:
  - L593: "As with radio and television ads, the disclosure should accompany the claim."
  - Audio claims need audio disclosures (L599). Written claims need written disclosures "not be
    placed solely in an audio or video clip" (L600).
  - L614: multiple media "does not alter the requirement ... in each advertisement that would
    require a disclosure if viewed in isolation."
- Language (L618-L620): "avoid legalese or technical jargon".

### Responsibility statements (who must do what)

- Advertisers:
  - L221: "Advertisers are responsible for ensuring that all express and implied claims that an ad
    conveys to reasonable consumers are truthful and substantiated."
  - L251: "Advertisers are responsible for ensuring that their messages are truthful and not
    deceptive."
  - L518: "It is the advertiser’s responsibility to draw attention to the required disclosures."
  - L483: "the advertiser disseminating it is not exempt".
  - L503: "Advertisers should employ best practices to make it less likely that disclosures will be
    deleted from space-constrained ads when they are republished by others."
- Platforms (L120, L253/L269): a platform that does not allow clear and conspicuous disclosures
  "should not be used". The advertiser is the one directed not to use it; the text places no duty on
  platform operators.
- Endorsers and bloggers:
  - Ex21, L856: "The blogger in this example obtained the paint she is reviewing for free and must
    disclose that fact."
  - Ex10: Julie Brown's "FS" icon for a free sample.
  - Ex14-17: JuliStarz as a paid endorser. Required disclosure (1) is "that JuliStarz is a paid
    endorser"; the text does not say who must make it beyond the ad itself.
- Website operators (n29, L557): "can identify visitors who are using mobile devices".
- Persons or companies that fail to comply with a guide (n5, L168): "the Commission might bring an
  enforcement action".
