answer: Three services would expose the provider to liability. They are the three examples in chapter 6 of the CMA's fake-reviews guidance (CMA208):
(1) setting up and running social-media groups to recruit people willing to post fake (or concealed incentivised) reviews of a trader's products;
(2) selling reviews that look consumer-written but were generated by software such as bots;
(3) a search engine optimisation (SEO) service that improves a trader's rating partly by generating fake reviews.
The chapter 7 facilitation services would not create liability under the rule: setting up accounts that avoid detection, and running a platform that knowingly allows fake-review services to be sold.

   - Intermediaries, including "review brokers, or reputation management companies", "could be liable under Section 465.2(a) if they write, create, or sell a fake or false consumer review…".    - "Brokers of fake reviews would generally fall under Section 465.2(a)'s prohibition against selling a consumer review, given that such brokers are generally being paid to provide fake reviews."

   - D01 §465.2(a)(1)-(2) covers reviews that misrepresent that the "reviewer… exists" or "used or otherwise had experience". §465.1(d) defines a consumer review to include "a purported consumer's evaluation".
