answer: Three of the five services would expose the provider to liability, all under §465.2(a):
(1) Setting up and running social-media groups to recruit people who will post fake (or concealed incentivised) reviews of a trader's products.
(2) Selling reviews that look as if individual consumers wrote them but were generated by bots or other software.
(3) An SEO service that raises a trader's rating partly by generating fake reviews.
The other two would not: (4) a service that sets up accounts so they avoid or reduce the risk of detection, and (5) running a platform or social-media site that knowingly allows traders to sell fake-review services for other sites.

1. The UK guidance is CMA208 (D04). It lists the services it bans in Ch. 6 and Ch. 7, five examples in all.
   - Ch. 6, D04:424: "It is prohibited to offer to submit or commission fake reviews or concealed incentivised reviews for traders… the business model of acting as a broker procuring banned reviews". Its examples:
     - D04:428: "setting up and running groups on social media to recruit individuals willing to post fake reviews or concealed incentivised reviews about a trader's products"
     - D04:430: "Selling reviews which look like they have been written by individual consumers but have in fact been generated by software applications (such as bots)"
     - D04:432: "search engine optimisation service that promises to improve a trader's rating, when one of the means that will be used is generating fake reviews"
   - Ch. 7, D04:444: "offer services to traders for the facilitating of…". Its examples:
     - D04:448-450: "enable accounts to be set up in a way that avoids or reduces the risk of detection"
     - D04:452: "Running an online platform or a social media site while being aware of and allowing services to be sold by traders… fake reviews… to be posted on other sites"
   - The UK bans the offer itself. The FTC rule covers actual conduct, which is why the question says "actually provided".
2. The FTC rule (D01):
   - §465.2(a), D01:132-147: a business may not "write, create, or sell a consumer review" that materially misrepresents "That the reviewer… exists", that the reviewer used the product, or their experience.
   - D01:101 defines a consumer review to include "a purported consumer's evaluation".
   - The other sections cover purchasing (465.2(b)), procuring reviews from insiders (465.2(c)), incentives tied to sentiment (465.4), insider reviews (465.5), company-controlled review sites (465.6), review suppression (465.7) and fake social-media metrics (465.8). None covers facilitating fake reviews or operating a platform.
3. The FTC staff Q&A (D03):
   - D03:63: "Brokers of fake reviews would generally fall under Section 465.2(a)'s prohibition against selling a consumer review, given that such brokers are generally being paid to provide fake reviews."
   - D03:31: "an individual in the business of selling or creating fake consumer reviews could be liable under Section 465.2(a)."
   - D03:35: review brokers and reputation-management companies "could be liable under Section 465.2(a) if they write, create, or sell a fake or false consumer review…" and under 465.4 if their incentives are conditioned on sentiment.
   - D03:71 and D03:83, on hosts: "not liable under the rule… assuming you didn't write or buy them"; there is no duty to investigate.
4. Applying this to each service:
   - Service 1 is the broker model CMA describes. The provider is paid to supply fake reviews, so this is "selling" under 465.2(a).
   - Service 2 sells reviews that falsely imply the reviewer exists or used the product, so 465.2(a) applies.
   - Service 3 has the provider writing or creating fake reviews, so 465.2(a) applies.
   - Service 4 only sets up accounts. The provider writes, creates, sells and buys no reviews, and no provision of the rule covers facilitation.
   - Service 5 is a platform operator that writes, creates, sells and buys no reviews. Staff say hosts are not liable for reviews they did not write or buy and have no duty to investigate.
