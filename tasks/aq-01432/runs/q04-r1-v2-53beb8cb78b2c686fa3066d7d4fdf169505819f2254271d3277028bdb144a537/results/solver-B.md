answer: Three services, all from chapter 6 of the UK guidance (CMA208, D04). (1) Setting up and running social-media groups that recruit people to post fake (or concealed-incentivised) reviews of a trader's products. (2) Selling reviews generated by bots and made to look as if individual consumers wrote them. (3) A search-engine-optimisation service, promising a better rating, that generates fake reviews. The two chapter 7 "facilitation" services would not expose the provider to liability under the rule: (4) services that let accounts be set up so they avoid or reduce detection, and (5) running a platform or social-media site that knowingly allows traders to sell services that post or arrange fake reviews on other sites.

D04 §6.1 (text/D04.txt lines 424-432): "It is prohibited to offer to submit or commission fake reviews or concealed incentivised reviews for traders… aimed at… the business model of acting as a broker procuring banned reviews". Its examples are 1 (social-media recruitment groups "willing to post fake reviews or concealed incentivised reviews"), 2 ("Selling reviews which look like they have been written by individual consumers but have in fact been generated by software applications (such as bots)") and 3 ("search engine optimisation service… when one of the means that will be used is generating fake reviews").

Its examples are 1 ("enable accounts to be set up in a way that avoids or reduces the risk of detection") and 2 ("Running an online platform or a social media site while being aware of and allowing services to be sold by traders… to post… fake reviews… on other sites"). D09 lines 561-573 confirm that banned practice 13 covers "Offering services to traders… or which facilitate any of those things", with "illustrative examples… provided in separate guidance".
3. D01 §465.2(a) makes it unlawful "for a business to write, create, or sell a consumer review… that materially misrepresents… (1) That the reviewer… exists; (2) That the reviewer… used or otherwise had experience…". Under §465.1(d), a consumer review includes "a purported consumer's evaluation". Under §465.1(a), a business includes "any other commercial entity that sells products or services".
4. D03 (FTC staff Q&A) says: "Brokers of fake reviews would generally fall under Section 465.2(a)'s prohibition against selling a consumer review, given that such brokers are generally being paid to provide fake reviews." It also says review brokers or reputation management companies "could be liable under Section 465.2(a) if they write, create, or sell a fake or false consumer review", and adds "§465.4 if they provide compensation or other incentives conditioned on… a particular sentiment". Separately: "an individual in the business of selling or creating fake consumer reviews could be liable under Section 465.2(a)."
5. How each service is classified:
   - (1) A paid broker supplying fake reviews counts as "selling" under §465.2(a), per the staff answer on brokers. §465.4 also applies if the recruits' rewards depend on positive sentiment.
   - (2) This is selling reviews that misrepresent that the reviewer exists or had experience, so §465.2(a) "sell" applies.
   - (3) This is creating fake reviews, so §465.2(a) "create" applies. Staff name reputation-management companies.
   - (4) and (5): no provision applies. D01's operative sections (§§465.2, 465.4-465.8) cover writing, creating or selling reviews; buying or disseminating them; getting reviews from insiders; incentives tied to sentiment; insider disclosure; company-controlled review sites; suppression; and fake social-media indicators. None covers facilitation or knowingly letting others sell such services.
   - Accounts are not "indicators of social media influence", which §465.1(j) defines as "any metrics… such as followers… likes… comments". So §465.8 does not reach (4).
   - In (5) the platform writes, creates and sells nothing, and the reviews appear on other sites. Staff also say hosts have no duty to investigate: "There is no such requirement or obligation under the rule."
