answer: Three services would expose the provider to liability. All three are from CMA208 Chapter 6, "Offering to procure banned reviews and information for traders":
(1) Setting up and running social-media groups that recruit people to post fake reviews about a trader's products. The provider is a fake-review broker "selling" consumer reviews under §465.2(a), and is also caught by §465.4 if it pays for reviews with a set sentiment.
(2) Selling bot-generated reviews made to look as if consumers wrote them. This is §465.2(a): selling a review that misrepresents that the reviewer exists.
(3) A search engine optimisation (SEO) service that promises a better rating and uses fake reviews to get it. This is §465.2(a): writing or creating fake consumer reviews, and the rule counts ratings as consumer reviews.
Neither Chapter 7 "facilitation" service would expose the provider to liability:
(4) a service that sets up accounts so they avoid detection;
(5) a platform that knowingly lets traders sell fake-review services on it.
The rule has no facilitation or hosting liability. The bases staff list for intermediaries do not reach either service.
