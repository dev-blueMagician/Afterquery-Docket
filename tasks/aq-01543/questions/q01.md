## Question

In calendar year 2024, what was coal's share of Poland's electricity generation minus coal's share of Bulgaria's, in percentage points to one decimal place?

## Answer

22.8 percentage points

## Components

- c1: 22.8 percentage points

## Reasoning

Step 1. Source input (Doc 2, Energy Sector, Power Generation): Poland's coal share of its electricity generation for the calendar year, "In 2024, the share of coal in electricity generation was 56.2%".

Step 2. Source input (Doc 1, Energy): Bulgaria's electricity production for the calendar year, which fell "to some 37,963 GWh in 2024".

Step 3. Source input (Doc 1, Energy): Bulgaria's output from coal and nuclear plants together for the calendar year, "Baseload power plants (coal, nuclear) generated a total of 28,46TWh of electricity in 2024", where the comma is a decimal comma.

Step 4. Calculation (Doc 1, unit conversion of the baseload total): 28,46 TWh = 28,460 GWh.

Step 5. Source input (Doc 1, Energy): Bulgaria's nuclear share for the calendar year, "In 2024, nuclear power accounted for approximately 41.6% of Bulgaria’s total electricity generation".

Step 6. Calculation (Doc 1, nuclear output from the nuclear share and the production total): 41.6% of 37,963 GWh = 15,792.6 GWh.

Step 7. Calculation (Doc 1, coal output, because the baseload total covers only coal and nuclear plants): 28,460 - 15,792.6 = 12,667.4 GWh.

Step 8. Calculation (Doc 1, Bulgaria's coal share of its production): 12,667.4 / 37,963 = 33.37%.

Step 9. Calculation (Doc 1 and Doc 2, Poland's coal share applied to Bulgaria's production, the coal output Bulgaria would need to match Poland's share): 56.2% * 37,963 = 21,335.206 GWh.

Step 10. Calculation (Doc 1 and Doc 2, how far Bulgaria's actual coal output falls short of that level): 21,335.206 - 12,667.4 = 8,667.806 GWh.

Step 11. Calculation (Doc 1 and Doc 2, the shortfall as a share of Bulgaria's production, which equals Poland's coal share minus Bulgaria's in percentage points): 8,667.806 / 37,963 = 22.83%.

Step 12. Final answer: the question asks for one decimal place, so 22.83% is rounded once, at this last step, to the gold answer in percentage points.

### Failure mode 1
Trap: Out-of-range period
How a careless solver fails: Uses the wrong period or date
Where the trap sits: Doc 1, Bulgaria's Energy chapter, the paragraph that opens "From September 2024 to August 2025, electricity consumption in Bulgaria relied primarily on low-carbon sources" and closes "Fossil fuels account for about 32.73% of the energy mix, with coal being dominant at 27.6% and natural gas providing a minimal 5.13%." It is the tenth paragraph of the Energy chapter, eight paragraphs after the one with the 2024 production figure, and it is the most detailed coal percentage in Doc 1, so a search that pairs "coal" with a percentage finds it. Its twelve-month window starts in September 2024 and runs eight months into 2025, and it is framed as the consumption mix rather than one calendar year's generation.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: 28.6 percentage points. The solver takes 27.6% as Bulgaria's coal share for 2024 and subtracts it from Poland's printed 56.2%: 56.2% - 27.6% = 28.6%. The answer overstates the gap by almost six points because it borrows a figure from a different twelve-month window.
Why the correct answer avoids it: The question asks about calendar year 2024. The paragraph's own opening words date the 27.6% to September 2024 through August 2025, a twelve-month window that is not a calendar year, so it is not a calendar-2024 figure. Doc 1 holds the calendar-2024 inputs in its opening paragraphs: production of 37,963 GWh, baseload output "(coal, nuclear)" of 28,46 TWh, and nuclear at about 41.6% of 2024 generation. Coal is baseload minus nuclear, 28,460 - 15,792.6 = 12,667.4 GWh, which is 33.37% of production, and 56.2% - 33.37% = 22.83%, which rounds to 22.8 percentage points.

### Failure mode 2

How a careless solver fails: Uses the wrong period or date
Where the trap sits: Doc 1, Bulgaria's Energy chapter. Doc 1 gives nuclear two numeric shares: "approximately 41.6%" of 2024 generation, credited to the IAEA in the Energy chapter's fifth paragraph (the one that opens "To support its energy needs"), and "about 40.38%" of electricity supply for September 2024 to August 2025, in the same paragraph as the 27.6% coal figure.
The wrong answer a careless solver gives: 21.6 percentage points. The solver sets up the right subtraction but takes the September to August nuclear share: coal plus nuclear is 28,460 / 37,963 = 74.97% of 2024 production, so coal comes out at 74.97% - 40.38% = 34.59%, and 56.2% - 34.59% = 21.61%, which rounds to 21.6. Why the correct answer avoids it: Both terms of the subtraction must come from calendar year 2024. The Energy chapter's fifth paragraph states the 2024 nuclear share directly: "In 2024, nuclear power accounted for approximately 41.6% of Bulgaria’s total electricity generation". With it, nuclear output is 15,792.6 GWh, coal output is 12,667.4 GWh, coal's share is 33.37%, and the gap from Poland's 56.2% is 22.8 percentage points.
