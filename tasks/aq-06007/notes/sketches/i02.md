## Question

Valuing each heavy-vehicle driver's gross value added at the national heavy-vehicle regulator's estimate, how much more output would Australia's truck-driver shortfall projected for 2029 cost than the 2024 shortfall in the study behind that projection? Answer in billions of dollars to one decimal place.

## Answer

$9.2 billion

## Evidence

- D02 (Doc 2 L491, Information request 3): "With each heavy vehicle driver contributing an estimated $184,000"
- D02 (Doc 2 L493): "This driver shortfall is projected to rise to 78,000"
- D02 (Doc 2 L504, footnote 16 to the 78,000): "International Road Transport Union (2024), 2024 Global Truck Driver Shortage Report."
- D10 (Doc 10 L360, Reform 3): "NatRoad, drawing on the 2024 Global Truck Driver Shortage Report by the International Road Transport Union (IRU), reports that in Australia 47% of drivers in 2024 were 55 or older, only 5.4% were under 25, and there were around 28,000 unfilled truck driving positions, projected to rise to over 78,000 by 2029"

Alternative source for the study's 2024 figure: D05 (Doc 5 L73, footnote 11 an iru.org link): "In 2024, around 28,000 truck driver positions remained unfilled"; D10 L782: "estimates that Australia has over 28,000 unfilled truck-driver positions today, with the shortfall projected to reach 78,000 by 2029". Settling text for the lure, not an input: D02 L481 "With an estimated 26,000 driver positions currently unfilled" with footnote 10 (L495) "NatRoad (2024), Truck driver shortage: Australia in the global context."
