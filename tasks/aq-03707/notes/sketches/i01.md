## Question

How far, in percentage points, is Foodstuffs North Island's 2015 to 2019 ROACE in the Commission's final report from the top of the range of overseas grocers' returns that Foodstuffs' profitability consultant treats as comparable with a return measured that way? Give one decimal place.

## Answer

2.1 percentage points above

## Evidence

- D08 (l172; Doc 1): "are 12.7% for Woolworths NZ, 12.8% for Foodstuffs SI and 13.1% for Foodstuffs NI"
- D08 (l178; Doc 1): "including the centralised functions provided by the co-operatives and ownership of property used for retail stores"
- D03 (para 3, l72; Doc 11): "to calculate those returns on the assumption that the stores are renters of the land and buildings assets that are employed"
- D03 (para 29b, l308; Doc 11): "calculated on an unlevered, whole of business basis"
- D03 (para 8b i, l159; Doc 11): "the average unlevered return on capital employed for the international sample of grocery retailers was between approximately 9 per cent and 11 per cent"
- D03 (para 8b ii, l162; Doc 11): "this range would adjust to approximately 22 per cent to 25 per cent to be consistent with the leverage"

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. Doc 11, written before the final report, says the Commission intends to measure Foodstuffs at store level with the stores assumed to be renters, calls that return highly levered and gives two ranges for overseas grocers: 9 to 11 per cent unlevered, 22 to 25 per cent at the renter leverage. Doc 1, the final report, measures Foodstuffs on a whole of business basis with the store property counted, which Doc 11 itself calls unlevered. The matching range is the first; 13.1 - 11 = 2.1 above its top.
Ambiguity: 11.9 below (top of 22 to 25) -> fails "measured that way": Doc 1 l178 says the final figure is whole of business with ownership of store property, and Doc 11 l308 calls that basis unlevered. 7.6 (Doc 1's own 5.5% normal return, l174) -> the question names the consultant's range of overseas grocers' returns. 4.1 (bottom, 9) -> "top". 1.7 or 1.8 (Woolworths NZ, Foodstuffs SI) -> "Foodstuffs North Island". NZX50 sector averages (Doc 11 l310-314) -> New Zealand listed firms, not overseas grocers. Deloitte's 3 to 3.5 per cent (Doc 11 l324) -> a margin the consultant rejects, not a return on capital. Tesco 6.85 and Kroger 15.16 (Doc 11 l342) -> single firms, not a range.
Necessity: Doc 1 -> the only document with 13.1% and with the final basis (searched "13.1", "ROACE", "whole of business", "WOB", "right of use", "renter" in all 13) -> without it the figure is unknown and Doc 11 alone points to the renter range. Doc 11 -> the only document with either range and the matching rule (searched "9 per cent", "11 per cent", "22 per cent", "25 per cent", "unlevered", "international sample") -> without it no range exists.
After clarification: both still needed; the wording supplies neither the figure, the basis nor a range, and avoids "unlevered", "leverage", "renter" and "whole of business".
Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; Same series, right edition (conference intention in Doc 11 l72 against the final report in Doc 1 l178). Wrong routes recomputed: 25 - 13.1 = 11.9; 13.1 - 5.5 = 7.6; 13.1 - 9 = 4.1.
Source risks: both ranges are "approximately"; 13.1% sits at character 22,308 of Doc 1, past the deepest position the form's number check has been seen to pass; the documents never set the final figure against the range themselves; the bundle overview prints both ranges with their labels.
Author calculation: Doc 1 l172: 13.1 (per cent, Foodstuffs NI, average of 2015 to 2019). Doc 11 l159: top of the unlevered overseas range 11 (per cent). 13.1 - 11 = 2.1 percentage points, above. Python 2026-10-12: round(13.1 - 11, 1) = 2.1.
