## Question

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: two companies print no tax rate. Hilton: pre-tax = net income + tax = 1,198 vs 1,039,
rate 27.8% vs 28.6% (fell), although D06 says tax expense rose (333 vs 297, +12%). Marriott: rate from
its rebuilt pre-tax income 1,902 vs 1,818 (all D08 six-month captions, as in i01): 25.7% vs 21.5%
(rose; D08 cites the prior-year release of tax reserves). Printed: Choice 27.2 vs 25.0 (rose), Wyndham
24.9 vs 23.2 (rose), Hyatt 37.2 vs 78.1 (fell).

Hard design (playbook kind): two independent judgments in different passages (playbook 4.6, screened
3/3 only before). J1: Hilton's rising tax expense is not a rising rate (decoy; wrong answer adds
Hilton). J2: Marriott's rate must be built from captions (wrong answer leaves it out). Default reading's
wrong answer: "Choice Hotels, Wyndham" (printed rates only), or with Hilton added.

Ambiguity: "effective income tax rate" = provision / income before income taxes (the companies that print
it use that ratio: Hyatt D04 l.336-338 182 / 73 = 40.3%). Hyatt's rate fell on any basis. Hilton prints
"Net income" (no attributable split), so pre-tax = 865 + 333.

Necessity: D02, D10, D04 printed rates; D06 Hilton's net income and tax; D08 Marriott's captions and
tax. Each company's status needs its Q2 document; the Q1 documents give only Q1. Delete-and-guess:
without D06 Hilton unknown; without D08 Marriott unknown; neither guess gives the gold reliably.

After clarification: five documents required.

Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: figure that must be computed (twice); decoy Hilton (tax expense up,
rate down). Counterfactual: printed rates only give Choice Hotels, Wyndham.

Source risks: shares the Marriott rebuild with i01 (set overlap; alternatives for the user, not a pair).
Hilton's margin 0.8 points, robust to rounding ($1M on 1,198).
