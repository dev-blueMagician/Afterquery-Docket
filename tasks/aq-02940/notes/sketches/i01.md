## Question

At the long-run percentage fall in merchant service fees per 1% interchange cut that a Spanish study found, what would the Commerce Commission's proposed commercial credit caps save merchants annually, in $ million to one decimal?

## Answer

$26.5 million

## Evidence

- D13 (Executive summary X4): "New Zealand merchants currently pay approximately $125 million per year in interchange fees to accept domestic and foreign-issued commercial credit cards."
- D13 (Executive summary X14): "we estimate the draft decision would reduce commercial credit interchange fees by $50 million."
- D13 (paragraph 1.4): "Merchants currently pay approximately $170 million in merchant service fees each year to accept Mastercard and Visa commercial credit card payments."
- D06 (section 5.2.3, pass-through): "one Spanish study estimates that a 1% reduction in interchange fees is associated in the long run with a 0.39% reduction in MSFs, suggesting partial rather than full pass-through."

## Sketch notes

Hook/mechanism: h1. A stated response rate (D06) applied to the other document's totals (D13), where the rate is a percentage of merchant service fees per percentage of interchange, so it must be applied to the fee base ($170 million) after turning the dollar cut into a percentage cut (50 / 125), not to the $50 million itself.
Ambiguity: (a) $40 million, the Commission's own estimate (D13 L105) -> fails "At the ... fall ... that a Spanish study found". (b) $19.5 million = 0.39 x $50 million -> fails "percentage fall in merchant service fees per 1% interchange cut": it treats 0.39 as a share of the dollar cut, which the study's sentence does not say (D06 L694, fn 172 L705 "0.39% lower MSC [or MSF]"); the question names both percentages so the percentage-point reading (1 point of transaction value -> 0.39 point) is excluded by its wording. (c) $29.1 million via the domestic rate cut 1.39% -> 0.78% -> fails "commercial credit caps" (domestic and foreign together; $170 million covers both). (d) $29.6 million via total interchange $125m / 19% and the $1 billion all-card fee bill (D11 L17) -> fails "commercial credit". Searched D01-D16 for "0.39", "Spanish", "pass-through", "$50 million", "170 million".
Necessity: D06 -> the 0.39% response (only at L694 and its footnote L705; no other document names the study or the figure) -> without it only the Commission's $40 million can be given. D13 -> the $50 million interchange reduction (only in D13; D10, D11 and D16 print the $40 million, and D11 reprints $125 million and $170 million) -> without it the percentage cut in interchange cannot be formed, since no other document gives the reduction in interchange or the Table 3.1 averages for both card origins.

Selected lever(s) and applicable tests: Calculate across documents; State the rounding. Wrong-route calculations: 0.39 x 50 = 19.5; 0.156 x 125 = 19.5; Commission 40; 0.39 x 40 = 15.6.

Author calculation: 50 / 125 = 0.4 (interchange cut as a share of commercial credit interchange); 0.4 x 0.39 = 0.156 (share of merchant service fees); 0.156 x 170 = 26.52 -> $26.5 million. Script check 2026-10-11 (fractions): 26.52.
