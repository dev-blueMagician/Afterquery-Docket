Bank of New Zealand's response to Commerce Commission's Commercial Credit Card Interchange Fee Regulation for Mastercard and Visa networks - Draft Decision and Reasons paper.

1 Introduction
1.1 BNZ welcomes the opportunity to respond to the Commerce Commission’s consultation on the Commercial Credit Card Interchange Fee Regulation for Mastercard and Visa networks – Draft Decision and Reasons paper. We support the Commission’s objective of promoting competition, transparency and fairer outcomes across the retail payments system.

1.2 BNZ notes that commercial credit cards are not simply consumer credit cards for business. They carry different fraud, servicing and administrative considerations. BNZ is broadly supportive of the introduction of caps on commercial cards, although in our view, the currently proposed level is too low and is not reflective of the specific characteristics of commercial cards.

1.3 Our submission is focused on three points:

1.3.1 Inclusion of other payment networks: the Commission should monitor for shifts to less-regulated payment schemes and consider regulating other payment systems where this results in higher merchant costs or acceptance pressure.
1.3.2 Simplicity for merchants: we recommend a minimum six‐month implementation period between the final decision and commencement to support effective implementation.
1.3.3 Consequences of lower interchange revenue: the Commission should clearly assess the downstream consequences of lower interchange revenue, including fraud, product sustainability, innovation and investment impacts.

2 Inclusion of other payment networks
2.1 BNZ encourages the Commission to consider the position of comparable payment networks as part of the final decision. We note that the draft decision recognises the risk that commercial cardholders may switch to alternative payment networks, to retain rewards and other benefits.

2.2 BNZ considers this a critical point. We believe that if the Commission reduces interchange revenue on Mastercard and Visa commercial credit cards, but leaves comparable schemes outside the regulatory perimeter, there is a clear risk that spend continues the shift towards less regulated or higher-cost payment systems. We believe that outcome may not necessarily reduce merchant costs overall, but instead may simply move, and even increase, costs from one part of the payment system to another.
2.3 We encourage the Commission to set out a clear approach to monitoring and, designate other payment systems and issue a comparable network standard where there is evidence of substitution, rising merchant costs, or increased acceptance pressure. We believe the goal should be a neutral framework that supports lower costs and better transparency across the payments ecosystem, not just within the Mastercard and Visa networks.

3 Simplicity for merchants
3.1 BNZ strongly supports simplicity and transparency in the retail payments system. This has been a consistent theme in our previous submission. We acknowledge the Commission's efforts to simply the interchange framework for Mastercard and Visa transactions. The proposal for commercial credit cards, if set at a sustainable level, together with aligning pre-paid card interchange settings, would substantially complete the remaining part of the interchange framework for those schemes.

3.2 Regarding implementation, we note the staged nature of the interchange reforms to date has required merchants and market participants to respond to multiple rounds of change, including pricing and communication impacts. BNZ supports timely implementation that is clean, clear, and consistent. The new proposed changes are likely to require scheme co-ordination, system updates, further pricing changes, governance, and communication to merchant customers. We strongly encourage the Commission to ensure the implementation period provides enough certainty for participants to apply the final settings properly and avoid unnecessary operational risk or inconsistent market outcomes.

3.3 To illustrate this, we refer to the greater merchant choice enabled by BNZ offering different merchant service fee pricing options. BNZ offers both 'interchange plus' and alternative pricing options. There is a considerable modelling effort to implement new regulatory caps for all merchants on alternative pricing. This usually involves around a six-month process from the point rates are published through to implementation. We also note that this may be the third recent repricing for some merchants, following repricing in December 2025 and May 2026. Therefore, BNZ strongly recommends a period of at least six months from the final decision date before the new settings take effect.

3.4 We also urge the Commission to avoid a pre-Christmas deployment to ensure participants can implement the changes in a controlled manner and not during the year's busiest trading period.

3.5 We believe the commencement date in clause 2 of the network standard should be amended to identify when the commercial credit card caps come into effect. The 2

3 present drafting of the draft Amendment Network Standard does not include placeholder drafting to insert a date for when the new caps would come into effect.
4 Consequences of lower interchange revenue
4.1 BNZ recognises that the Commission is seeking to reduce merchant service fees and improve outcomes for merchants and consumers. However, we believe that lower interchange revenue is not without consequence. We strongly suggest that the final decision paper clearly considers the downstream effects of materially reducing interchange revenue for commercial credit card products.

4.2 We note that these products involve different customer needs, use cases, risk considerations, and serving requirements from consumer credit cards. BNZ is concerned that the proposed caps are so low that they may affect issuer incentives to maintain and invest in commercial card functionality, fraud controls, and customer support.
4.3 BNZ considers the final decision would benefit from more directly addressing these potential consequences:

4.3.1 Whether lower interchange revenue could affect investment in fraud prevention and security controls;

4.3.2 Whether commercial card product features, reporting, controls, or servicing could be affected over time;

4.3.3 Whether costs may shift to business cardholders through fees;

4.3.4 Whether issuers will retain sufficient incentive to invest in commercial card functionality;

4.3.5 Whether substitution to other payment systems could reduce or offset expected merchant benefits; and
4.3.6 Whether small businesses that rely on commercial cards for business purchasing could face reduced functionality or increased direct costs.

5 Conclusion
5.1 BNZ supports the Commerce Commission’s objective of promoting a more efficient, transparent and competitive payments system. We are broadly supportive of appropriate commercial credit card interchange caps and encourage the Commission to ensure a consistent regulatory framework across comparable payment networks, allow sufficient time for implementation, and give careful consideration to the broader impacts of reduced interchange revenue on innovation, product availability and sustainability, and business customers. We appreciate the opportunity to provide feedback and look forward to continuing to engage with the Commission on the Retail Payment System Act 2022.
